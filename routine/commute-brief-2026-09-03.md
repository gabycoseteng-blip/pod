## OP-EDS & COMMENTARY

PRIMARY: **Elliott Abrams** (CFR, former Trump Venezuela envoy), WaPo Opinion, "The
Venezuela oil deal Trump orchestrated is a shameful sellout" (Sept 2) — argues Trump
partnered with "the most shadowy and repellent Venezuelan businessman he could find"
after major oil majors wouldn't put up capital, legitimizing an unelected
Chavista-linked government: "the great sellout — of Venezuela's natural resources and
its people's right to self-government."
COUNTER-TAKE: companion WaPo piece same day argues the opposite failure — the deal,
struck with Maduro's *former* VP eight months after his ouster, entrenches the same
officials/policies that wrecked the economy, squandering the reform window a pro-market
opposition was ready to fill. Push further: Abrams spent years on the maximum-pressure
sanctions strategy that also never produced a democratic transition — his
"self-government" framing deserves scrutiny too. The deal is ugly but is also the first
arrangement moving Venezuelan barrels and lowering prices; the purist wait-for-a-clean-
partner alternative has had years to materialize and hasn't.
BACKUP: Mark Leonard, Foreign Affairs, "The Abandoned Order" — the rules-based order
isn't being overthrown so much as abandoned by an American public that's lost interest
(Trump as accelerant, not cause); result is a "rotten-tail world" of scavenged
fragments. Counter: understates how load-bearing the dollar/SWIFT/US security
guarantees remain — the framing risks being self-fulfilling.
