## OP-EDS & COMMENTARY (1-2 pieces, steelman + counter-take)
PRIMARY: wooldridge-bloomberg-tech-mogul-strongman — Adrian Wooldridge,
Bloomberg Opinion, "The Tech Mogul Strongman Is On the Rise" (Sept 4).
Steelman: the 19th-century liberal order rested on twin revolutions — political
(constraining rulers via constitutions) and corporate (public companies
answerable to dispersed shareholders, not a founder-king). Wooldridge argues
both are reversing in tandem: strongman politics has an exact corporate
mirror in founder-controlled tech giants — dual-class shares, subservient
boards. Concretely: Alphabet founders hold 51.3% of voting rights, Meta's
Zuckerberg 57.9%, Musk 85% of SpaceX's voting power — "public capital, private
control." Argues the business-side reversal may prove MORE durable than the
political strongman trend, rewiring accountability structures a century in the
making with far less public scrutiny than a Putin or Orbán gets. Counter-take:
corporate-governance critics reject the "founders need insulation from
short-term markets" framing — "one share, one vote" is shareholder democracy's
actual cornerstone, and decoupling economic risk from voting control is naked
entrenchment dressed as far-sighted stewardship (the "founder mythology"
critique). Rebuttal: shareholders enter these arrangements voluntarily and
with disclosed terms at IPO, unlike citizens under an actual autocrat — the
tidy political/corporate parallel overstates the analogy for effect.
