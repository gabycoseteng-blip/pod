(~600,000 students, two-thirds of enrollment) starting this school year, paired with
screen-time caps (30 min/day grades 3-5, 45 min middle school) and a device ban below

limits on exactly the population least equipped to self-regulate use — cuts against
both the ed-tech industry's push and how most districts nationally have handled it.
Source: WaPo, Chalkbeat, K12 Dive, NYC Mayor's Office, ABC7NY.

## OP-EDS & COMMENTARY

PRIMARY: **Joseph Stiglitz**, Project Syndicate (Sept 2), "The Twilight of US Global
Influence" — argues Trump/Bessent's escalating threats (tariffs, secondary
sanctions, dollar weaponization) are evidence of waning American leverage, not
strength — coercion is what powers reach for once persuasion and legitimacy erode.
The resulting multipolar transition, he argues, is healthier: it forces
burden-sharing and reduces the world's exposure to one erratic actor.
COUNTER-TAKE: this week's own throughline is "everyone underwriting for a fight they
can't fully control" — Hormuz incidents spreading to four countries, an
oil-legitimacy trade in Venezuela, PBOC struggling to manage a currency it can't
fully control. A receding US hegemon doesn't obviously produce Stiglitz's calmer
multipolarity — historically, waning single-power orders (interwar Europe,
post-Ottoman Middle East) produce vacuums filled by regional powers with less
capacity and less accountability to coordinate, not more.
BACKUP: **Jan-Werner Mueller**, Project Syndicate (Sept 3) — argues populist parties
(AfD) win less through explicit policy argument than through style/aesthetic
signaling that lets voters absorb exclusionary politics without confronting it as
such. Counter: the aesthetics framing risks being unfalsifiable and elitist-coded —
it can imply voters are being duped by branding rather than making a legible
judgment, which risks the very "who gets to say what's real politics" gatekeeping
that fuels AfD support in the first place.
