## SEGMENT SEVEN — MANDARIN VOCAB

SAM: Today's two Mandarin words come from the China story, and both are about consequence and time. The first is 于是, yúshì, a connective meaning "so" or "thereupon."
ALEX: 于是 narrates what happened next, as a reaction. It sits in written register but is perfectly natural in speech. Here is an example. 房地产销售持续低迷，于是地方政府不得不重新考虑土地出让的节奏。 (Property sales stayed depressed, so local governments had to rethink the pace of land sales.)
SAM: The near-synonyms are 因此 and 所以, and the difference is worth knowing. 因此 and 所以 state a logical result, a cause and its conclusion. 于是 tells a story in sequence, so the result follows in time and is often an action someone chose.
ALEX: Right, so 所以 answers "why," while 于是 answers "and then what." Another example: 价格涨得太快，于是消费者开始观望。 (Prices rose too fast, and so consumers started to wait and see.) You would not swap 于是 for 所以 in a case where the second clause is not a reaction.
SAM: The second word is 至今, zhìjīn, meaning "up to now" or "to this day." It is 书面语, written or formal register, and it most often pairs with 仍 or 没, to stress that a hoped-for change has not arrived.
ALEX: The spoken equivalent is 到现在. For example, 北京的刺激措施偏重供给，至今仍未见到能明显拉动居民消费的举措。 (Beijing's stimulus measures lean toward supply, and to this day there is no sign of a measure that clearly lifts household consumption.)
SAM: Another one: 这家公司至今没有公布具体的数字。 (The company still has not released specific figures.) The collocation to remember is 至今 plus 仍, 没, or 未.
ALEX: Both appear in the lead sentence of the China segment, so listen for them.
