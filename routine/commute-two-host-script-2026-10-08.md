## SEGMENT THREE — DEEP DIVE

SAM: For the deep dive we are leaving the markets entirely. On Tuesday the Nobel committee gave the 2026 Physics prize to Francis Halzen of the University of Wisconsin–Madison, for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin.
ALEX: And it is a prize for an engineering gamble as much as a physics idea. IceCube is a cubic kilometer of ice at the South Pole, instrumented with 5,160 light sensors lowered into holes melted deep into the glacier, with the deepest roughly 2 kilometers down. The ice itself is the detector.
SAM: Walk through why you would build that. Neutrinos barely interact with anything, so you need a staggering amount of target material to catch even a rare few.
ALEX: Exactly. A neutrino passes through the Earth and almost never touches anything. Very occasionally it collides with a nucleus in the ice and produces a charged particle that moves faster than light does in ice, which emits a faint blue flash called Cherenkov light. The sensors record that flash, and from the timing across thousands of sensors you can reconstruct the direction and energy of the original neutrino.
SAM: And direction is the whole point. Halzen was born in Tienen, Belgium in 1944, spent time at CERN, joined Wisconsin in 1972, and by his own account proposed this style of detector in 1988, and has been the principal investigator since 2001. So this is a 38-year idea.
ALEX: Which is the context for why the prize reads as a lifetime one. The collaboration is roughly 450 scientists at 58 institutions in 14 countries, but the Nobel citation goes to the person who carried the vision through the decades when it looked like a detector that might never see anything.
SAM: What changed the story was September 22nd, 2017. A neutrino of roughly 290 trillion electron-volts arrived, and within minutes the alert went out to telescopes worldwide.
ALEX: And it pointed at a blazar named TXS 0506+056, about 4 billion light-years away in the constellation Orion, which happened to be in a gamma-ray flare. Archival digging then found a 3-month burst of 13 neutrinos from the same patch of sky in 2014 and 2015. That is the first credible identification of a specific source of a high-energy cosmic neutrino.
SAM: Why does that matter beyond the one source? Because cosmic rays, the charged particles that rain on the Earth, have been a mystery for 114 years. We have known they exist since 1912 and could not say where they come from.
ALEX: And the reason is magnetism. Cosmic rays are charged, so galactic and intergalactic magnetic fields bend their paths, and by the time they arrive they point nowhere useful. Neutrinos are neutral and almost non-interacting, so they travel in straight lines. A neutrino pointing at TXS 0506 is effectively a pointer at where the accelerator is.
SAM: There was a second landmark in 2022, when IceCube reported evidence of neutrinos from the active galaxy NGC 1068, also known as M77, about 47 million light-years away.
ALEX: That one is arguably more interesting scientifically. NGC 1068 has a dense, dust-shrouded core, so gamma rays are absorbed there, and you would see nothing in conventional light. Neutrinos escape. It suggests there are obscured accelerators that gamma-ray astronomy is blind to, which is the real new-astronomy claim.
SAM: And the detector has also now mapped neutrinos coming from our own Milky Way, and recorded events with energies reaching into the petaelectronvolt range, with the highest reported around 11.4 petaelectronvolts.
ALEX: Now the honest caveat, which is that the central puzzle is still open. Neither TXS 0506 nor NGC 1068 explains most of the diffuse flux of astrophysical neutrinos. They are single clues, not the full accounting of where the neutrinos come from.
SAM: Halzen has said himself that the way forward is more neutrino observations, since only populations of events will expose obscured cores. And the detector is being upgraded, with a larger successor planned.
ALEX: There is a lesson for anyone who works on long-duration infrastructure. This was a project whose payoff was uncertain for decades, funded in tranches, and the key choice was to make the environment itself the instrument. The 2017 event worked because the alert network was in place, and people could point other telescopes within minutes. The detector alone would not have produced the result.
SAM: And the prize structure is its own story. This is described as the first solo physics laureate in 34 years, which we are taking from the committee's coverage, in a field where the prize is usually split among three.
ALEX: In a collaboration of 450 people, a single-name prize is a statement about the role of the visionary. It also keeps the full 12 million Swedish kronor. The practical takeaway for the listener is the pattern: the biggest discoveries in this field now come from instruments that took 30 years to build and then ran quietly until the universe obliged.

## SEGMENT SEVEN — MANDARIN VOCAB

SAM: Today's two Mandarin words both come from the US-China tariff lists, and both are formal connectors or verbs you will hear on finance and policy desks. The first is 至于, zhìyú.
ALEX: 至于 is a topic-shifting connective meaning "as for" or "as to." It introduces a related but separate point after you have finished the previous one. It sits in neutral to written register, so you will see it in both speech and reports. Here is an example. 关税清单已经公布，至于具体税率，目前还没有消息。 (The tariff lists have been published; as for the specific rates, there's no word yet.)
SAM: And a contrast to keep in mind, since we taught 以至于 yesterday.
ALEX: Right, 以至于 means "so much so that" and introduces a result, while 至于 shifts the topic. They share characters but not function. The other near-synonym is 关于, which means "about" and frames the whole sentence's topic, whereas 至于 picks up a sub-topic partway through. A second example: 公司同意了价格，至于交货时间，还要再谈。 (The company agreed on the price; as for the delivery time, that still needs to be discussed.)
SAM: There is also a negative use, where 至于 means "to go so far as," almost always in questions and negatives. 他不至于这么生气吧。 (He surely wouldn't get this angry.)
ALEX: The second word is 落实, luòshí, which means to carry out, to put into effect, or to follow through. It is official register, extremely common in policy news. It collocates with 政策, 协议, and 措施. For example, 协议能不能真正落实，还要看双方的国内程序。 (Whether the agreement is truly implemented depends on each side's domestic procedures.)
SAM: And the near-synonym?
ALEX: 执行, which means to execute, and tends to sound mechanical, an order being carried out. 落实 implies making something real on the ground, and often implies it is a problem that something announced has not yet been done. Another example: 政府承诺的补贴还没有落实。 (The subsidies the government promised have not yet been put into effect.)
SAM: Keep those 2 in mind, because the lead sentence of the China segment uses both.
