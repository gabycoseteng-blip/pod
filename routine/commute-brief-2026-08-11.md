## 6. DEEP DIVE — source: INBOX (SemiAnalysis)
semianalysis-tilert-gpu-vs-asic-inference: SemiAnalysis, "Ultra-High Interactivity on NVIDIA GPUs? – TileRT InferenceX" (received Aug 10). Core argument: TileRT is a software layer — not new silicon — that closes most of the interactivity gap between commodity NVIDIA GPUs and purpose-built low-latency ASICs (Cerebras, Groq LPU, SambaNova) by replacing thousands of sequential kernel launches with a single statically-compiled "persistent Engine Kernel" that stays resident on-GPU for the whole decode lifecycle. Roofline math: an 8×B200 node (64 TB/s HBM) should theoretically hit ~3,047 tok/s/user on GLM-5 at batch=1 — real engines fall far short because the bottleneck is kernel-launch latency, not bandwidth (HBM bandwidth has grown 2-3x/generation; memory latency hasn't moved). Benchmarks on a single B200 8-GPU node: 340 tok/s/user at 8k/1k input/output (1.9x the prior best, GB300 NVL72 at 181.4 tok/s/user); 494.2 tok/s/user at 1k/1k FP8 (3.6x the prior best conventional FP8 engine); end-to-end latency beats the prior-best GLM-5.1 setup by 4.5x at 1k/1k. Iso-cost, TileRT runs ~2x more interactive than traditional engines. Tradeoff: batch-size-1 only — 160.4 tok/s/GPU throughput vs. GB300's 240 tok/s/GPU at 12-way concurrency ("a single-passenger rocketship"). Already in production: Xiaomi's MiMo V2.5 Pro UltraSpeed and Z.ai's GLM-5.1 HighSpeed, both via vLLM-prefill/TileRT-decode disaggregation — no new hardware procured. Author's take: GPU fungibility beats ASIC purity economically — a provider can carve a "speed tier" out of a fleet it already owns and reallocate the prefill/decode ratio via a config file, while an ASIC fleet locks in a fixed speed-capacity ratio at purchase that takes months to re-rack. Squeezes Cerebras/Groq/SambaNova's TAM from below, though the SRAM roofline still wins on raw ceiling and small dense models.

## 11. OP-EDS & COMMENTARY
- PRIMARY authers-abbey-aindicators-credit-doubt: John Authers & Richard Abbey, Bloomberg Opinion, "AIndicators Hint at Doubts in Credit Markets" (Aug 10) — steelman: launches a new "AIndicators" framework tracking AI-buildout risk through credit markets rather than equities, arguing lenders' willingness to keep financing data centers/chips/power is the real tell; draws the 19th-century railroad-bond parallel (Jay Cooke/Northern Pacific) — transformative tech still needs financing to outrun the crisis, and early credit-market readings already show doubt creeping in even as equities hit records. Counter: the 1873 railroad panic came from over-leveraged speculative lines, not the technology itself, and railroads eventually paid off — today's hyperscaler capex (Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, Google) is funded mostly by fortress balance sheets and free cash flow, not junk debt, weakening the parallel; credit-spread "doubt" may just as easily reflect Fed-policy uncertainty as AI-specific fear.
- PAIRED kashyap-wapo-fed-explain-decisions: Anil Kashyap, guest column, Washington Post, "The Fed should explain its decisions" (Aug 7) — steelman: Warsh rattled markets with vague "determined to hit target" language and no operational roadmap after five years of inflation misses, and with reports of direct Trump-Warsh calls breaking, the Fed needs to over-communicate to defend its independence.
