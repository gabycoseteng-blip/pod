## SEGMENT THREE — DEEP DIVE

SAM: Today's deep dive leaves markets and politics entirely. It's built on Nature's news coverage and Retraction Watch, and it's about the plumbing of science itself. On October 23, the engineering society IEEE retracted at least 1,417 conference papers in a single day, which Nature calls a record for one-day retractions.
ALEX: The trigger is a study titled Opening Pandora's Box: Paper Mills in Conference Proceedings, by Anna Abalkina and colleagues, published in Accountability in Research. Here's the method. The team collected more than 4,000 advertisements across over 200 platforms, Telegram, WhatsApp, Facebook, where paper mills openly sell authorship slots on papers that are about to be published. Then they matched those ads against IEEE conference papers from 2021 to 2026.
SAM: And the match was damning. They flagged 2,062 papers across 366 proceedings. By Nature's count, roughly 1,622 of those are now retracted, and IEEE has retracted close to 3,000 papers in 2026 alone. India is linked to 1,263 of the 1,417 in the Oct 23 batch, and 95 of the 109 conferences with high match rates were held in India, though reports differ on the exact geography.
ALEX: I want to dwell on what the retraction actually proves. IEEE's own statement came five days later, on October 28, and it did not name the papers. The cleanup did not come from the publisher's internal quality control. It came from outside sleuths handing matched lists to the publisher, and the publisher acting only when the evidence was undeniable.
SAM: There's a companion dataset called BuyTheBy, with 18,710 paper-mill ads and 51,812 timestamped prices. That price series is remarkable, because it turns an invisible shadow market into something you can chart. Authorship slots have a market price, and it moves.
ALEX: Which is the sharpest framing of the problem. If a byline is a commodity with a price, then it's the same economics as any other market with a quality problem: demand comes from incentives, like publish-or-graduate rules and promotion metrics, and supply comes from anyone who can make a paper look plausible.
SAM: Also this month, the National Library of Medicine put MEDLINE indexing on hold for Scientific Reports, from Springer Nature, pending re-evaluation, which was flagged by an independent researcher using PubMed data. That's not a paper-mill finding, but it shows the indexers, not the publishers, are becoming the gatekeepers.
ALEX: Let me give the contested points, because this story has real disagreements. First: does retraction at scale fix anything? Conferences are a weaker peer-review venue than journals to begin with, so a record batch could be cleaning the easiest target while the journals stay untouched. Second: the India-centric framing. The data are real, but critics argue the structural cause is global, universities everywhere that count papers.
SAM: Third, detection by ad-matching only catches mills that advertise. The tail that worries researchers is AI-generated papers with no advertisement trail. Retraction Watch is running an essay this week titled I Used AI to Fake a Scientific Paper, and the point of that title is that the cost of a plausible fake is collapsing toward zero.
ALEX: Fourth, mega-journal economics. When publishers earn per accepted paper, volume is revenue, and the pressure to accept is built in. That doesn't make any individual publisher corrupt, but it does mean the incentives and the integrity function sit in the same company.
SAM: Why does it matter to this audience? Because of the AI bridge. Large models train on the scientific literature and cite it. If a chunk of that corpus is purchased authorship and fabricated results, the contamination flows downstream into every system that treats a citation as a signal of reliability.
ALEX: And the second-order read is about who the next gatekeeper is. If NLM, Scopus and Web of Science start delisting venues, indexing becomes the enforcement layer, and publishers will need to show provenance rather than assert it. A retraction is a patch. A delisting is a policy.
SAM: Our caveat: we worked from news coverage and summaries of the study, not the full paper, so the exact counts are the figures as reported.
ALEX: The takeaway is simple. A record retraction sounds like the system working, but the system only moved when someone outside it published a list.

## SEGMENT SEVEN — MANDARIN VOCAB

ALEX: Two words today, both pulled from the China story we're about to hear. The first is 不过, which means "however" or "but."
SAM: It's a connective listeners at HSK 3 tend to treat as a straight swap for 但是, and the difference is register and weight. 不过 is spoken and softer. It concedes a point and then gently turns, where 但是 makes a firmer, more formal contrast.
ALEX: Here's the pattern. First clause states the fact, then 不过 introduces the qualification. 华发的债券大幅下跌，不过公司仍然在偿还部分债务。(Huafa's bonds fell sharply, however the company is still repaying part of its debt.)
SAM: Contrast it with 然而, which was covered on an earlier show. 然而 is written language, the kind you see in a finance column. 不过 is what you'd say aloud in the meeting right after.
ALEX: There's also a softening use at the end of a sentence's setup, as in 我同意你的看法，不过还有一个问题。(I agree with your view, but there is one more problem.) Notice it never cancels the first half the way 但是 can.
SAM: The second word is 仍然, meaning "still" or "nonetheless." It's formal-neutral and it signals that a situation persisted despite a change or a pressure.
ALEX: It's close to 依然 in meaning, and the two are nearly interchangeable. The difference is that 仍然 is a touch more common in written and spoken news, while 依然 leans literary. Use 仍然 when you want the plain reporting voice.
SAM: Collocation: 仍然 sits right before the verb, and it often follows a 虽然 or 不过 clause. 市场担心增长放缓，投资者仍然看好科技股。(The market worries about slowing growth, yet investors are still bullish on tech stocks.)
ALEX: Put them together and you get the shape of a lot of financial reporting. 价格下跌了，不过投资者仍然愿意持有。(Prices fell, however investors are still willing to hold.) One more, with a negative: 公司仍然没有公布具体数字。(The company still hasn't released the specific figures.)
SAM: One caution. 仍然 doesn't go at the front of a sentence the way 不过 can. If you put 仍然 first, the sentence feels unfinished.
