## SEGMENT THREE — DEEP DIVE

SAM: Time for the one thing we chewed on today, and it comes from far outside markets. This week's Economist, in its Science section, runs a piece on what astronomers call little red dots, under a headline along the lines of: astronomers are mystified by them, and they could be the universe's first supermassive black holes. We only have the headline and subhead from the newsletter, so the substance here is rebuilt from the underlying research and coverage, not from the article itself.

ALEX: Start with the observation. The James Webb Space Telescope keeps finding compact, very red points of light in the early universe, from when the cosmos was well under a billion years old. They are small, they are numerous, and they were essentially unknown before Webb. Whether they are dense clusters of stars or black holes has been argued about for years, and there is still no agreement.

SAM: And why red? The early assumption was dust, the usual cause of reddening. But Jorryt Matthee's team, which has argued for a baby-quasar reading, says the colour is better explained by hydrogen gas than by dust, and that the central black holes would weigh in at roughly 10 million to 100 million solar masses.

ALEX: That is where the trouble starts. If you take the black-hole interpretation at face value, the black holes look oversized for their host galaxies. A typical local black hole is a small fraction of a percent of its host's stellar mass, and the figure quoted for these objects runs around 0.1%, which is hard to reconcile with that many of them existing that early.

SAM: The second problem is that they are quiet. A growing supermassive black hole should shine in X-rays and often in radio, and for little red dots the expected X-ray and radio emission has simply not been detected. If these are quasars, they are behaving like quasars with the lights off.

ALEX: There are two families of fixes. One says the black holes are wrapped in so much gas that high-energy radiation is absorbed before it escapes. The other says they are feeding at unusual, super-Eddington rates, which would also change the spectrum. Both are physically plausible and both are hard to test.

SAM: The idea getting the most traction is the so-called black hole star. A 2025 preprint argues that if you cocoon a black hole in a dense, ionised gas envelope, the nebular emission from that cocoon dominates the optical spectrum and explains most of the features people were puzzling over. In that picture, the object looks less like a galaxy with a black hole and more like a star-like thing powered by one.

ALEX: There are two specific objects people keep pointing at. One is nicknamed The Cliff, studied by Anna de Graaff's team, with a steep spectral rise and a sharp Balmer break that fits neither leading story cleanly. The team describes it as probably the clearest black-hole-star example found so far, and follow-up Webb observations are scheduled for next year.

SAM: The other is an object in the GLIMPSE survey, catalogued as GLIMPSE-17775, which shows more than 40 spectral lines, many of which independently support a black hole in a hot, dense gas cocoon. A single object with that many cross-checks is more persuasive than a statistical argument over dozens of fuzzy ones.

ALEX: And then there is the fight over how universal this is. Rohan Naidu's group argues that every little red dot is consistent with being a black hole star embedded in an ordinary early galaxy. That is a strong claim, and it's contested. One catalogue assembled by a Max Planck Institute group, led by Raphael Hviding, found 35 of them, which is a decent sample, though far from the whole story.

SAM: So why does this belong on a markets and business show? I'd offer 2 reasons. The first is the second-order read on how science gets funded and believed. The same instrument produced both the observation and the controversy, and the controversy is the point. A 6.5-metre space telescope that costs about $10 billion over its life is generating exactly the kind of "wait, that can't be right" result that justifies building it.

ALEX: The second reason is the structure of the argument, which is a useful template for any big claim. There is a leading hypothesis, there is an object that nearly proves it, there is a prediction, and there is a scheduled test. If the black hole star model is right, there may be a new class of astronomical object, and the story of how black holes seeded and grew in the first few hundred million years has to be rewritten.

SAM: I'd flag the caution too. As of now there is no consensus, and I haven't seen a 2026 paper that settles it. So the right way to say it is: leading hypothesis, not settled science. The Economist headline hedges with could, and so should we.

ALEX: And one practical note for the commute. We've covered Rubin and a dwarf planet recently, so this is a different corner of astronomy, early-universe black holes rather than the outer solar system. If you want a single image to keep, it is a black hole wrapped in gas, hiding in plain sight at a redshift where nothing that big should exist yet.

## SEGMENT FIVE — TAGALOG VOCAB

SAM: Dalawang salita ang pag-aaralan natin ngayong umaga, at pareho itong may kinalaman sa balita sa Pilipinas na susunod. Ang una ay "pagkaltas."

ALEX: Ang "pagkaltas" ay galing sa salitang-ugat na "kaltas," na ibig sabihin ay ang pagbabawas o pagkuha ng bahagi mula sa kabuuan. Kapag may kaltas sa sahod, may bahaging hindi mo na mahahawakan dahil binawas na ito bago pa ibigay sa iyo.

SAM: Sa balita ngayon, ginagamit ito para sa badyet. Ang pagkaltas ng mahigit P8 bilyong halaga ng mga proyekto ng DPWH sa panukalang badyet para sa 2027 ay hindi simpleng pagbabawas. May dating itong paglilinis, na parang inaalis ang hindi dapat nandoon.

ALEX: Pansinin ang pagkakaiba nito sa "pagbawas" at sa "pagtanggal." Ang "pagbawas" ay pangkalahatan, puwedeng kaunti lang ang naalis. Ang "pagtanggal" ay buo ang pag-alis. Ang "pagkaltas" ay nasa gitna: may bahaging inaalis, at kadalasan may opisyal na dahilan o proseso sa likod nito.

SAM: Halimbawa: "Kinaltas ng Senado ang mga proyektong walang kumpletong dokumento." Pormal ang tunog nito at madalas mong maririnig sa mga ulat tungkol sa pananalapi at pamahalaan, hindi sa karaniwang kuwentuhan sa kanto.

ALEX: Ang ikalawang salita ay "sumigla." Galing ito sa "sigla," ang lakas at sigasig ng isang tao o bagay. Kapag sinabing "sumigla" ang isang bagay, bumangon ito mula sa pagkahina at nagkaroon ng bagong sigla.

SAM: Kaya ang "sumigla ang piso" ay may kasamang damdamin, hindi lang numero. Parang nabuhayan ang merkado. Ihambing mo ito sa "tumaas," na tuwid na pagtaas lang at walang kulay.

ALEX: Puwede rin itong gamitin sa tao: "Sumigla ang lolo nang dumating ang mga apo." Doon makikita ang init at pagiging personal ng salita, kaya sa balita ay nagbibigay ito ng kulay na hindi kayang ibigay ng "tumaas."

SAM: Sa madaling sabi, ang "pagkaltas" ay pagbawas na may dahilan at proseso, at ang "sumigla" ay muling pagbangon at pagkakaroon ng lakas. Pareho mo silang maririnig agad sa susunod na bahagi.

## SEGMENT SEVEN — MANDARIN VOCAB

SAM: Two Mandarin words today, both tied to the China story that follows. The first is 尤其, which means especially or in particular.

ALEX: 尤其 is a connective that singles out one item from a group, and it almost always appears as 尤其是 followed by a noun. It is neutral to slightly formal, and it's a good one for the listener's weak spot, advanced connectives, because it signals a ranking within a statement rather than just adding to it. The everyday near-synonym is 特别, but 特别 mostly means very, while 尤其 means this one more than the others.

SAM: An example. 市场整体下跌,尤其是科技股。(The market fell overall, especially tech stocks.) And another from the trade desk. 这家公司的出口增长很快,尤其是对美国的出口。(This company's exports are growing quickly, especially exports to the United States.)

ALEX: The second word is 繁荣, prosperous or booming. It is written-register, 书面语, and it pairs with big abstract nouns like 经济, 市场 and 文化. Its near-synonym 兴旺 is more colloquial and tends to describe a business or a family, so a restaurant is 兴旺, while a whole economy is 繁荣. The opposite is 萧条, which is slump or depression.

SAM: Example. 高科技出口的繁荣掩盖了国内需求的疲软。(The boom in high-tech exports masks the weakness in domestic demand.) Notice it works as a noun there, and it can also be an adjective, as in 经济繁荣, a prosperous economy.

ALEX: One more with the connective doing the heavy lifting. 尽管出口很繁荣,居民消费还是不振,尤其是在三线城市。(Even though exports are booming, household consumption is still weak, especially in third-tier cities.) You'll hear both words in the China segment next.
