## SEGMENT FOUR — VOCAB OF THE DAY

SAM: Vocab time — two Mandarin, two Tagalog, and today's pair up almost too neatly with the theme of the whole show.

ALEX: 没错，今天想聊两个词，都跟"事情看起来解决了，其实还没有"这个主题有关。第一个是一个句型 — "一方面……另一方面"。

SAM: 一方面……另一方面 — on one hand, on the other hand.

ALEX: 今天的市场就是最好的例子。一方面，标普和纳斯达克都创下新高，帕兰泰尔的财报也让投资人重新买入AI股；另一方面，德黑兰否认了霍尔木兹海峡的框架协议，油价随时可能重新反弹。(Today's market is the perfect example. On one hand, the S&P and Nasdaq both hit new highs, and Palantir's earnings brought investors back into AI stocks; on the other hand, Tehran denies the Hormuz framework exists, and oil could snap back at any time.)

SAM: 这个句型好像挺常用的，是不是比较正式？

ALEX: 算是中性偏正式，财经报道和新闻分析里特别常见，因为它天生就适合摆出两个对立的事实，而不做评判。跟它意思相近的还有"虽然……但是"，不过那个更强调转折、有一点让步的意味；一方面……另一方面更平衡，只是单纯陈述两个同时存在的现实，没有谁压过谁的感觉。今天这期节目里，中国的股市和中国的制造业数据就是最典型的一方面另一方面 — 股市在涨,可是制造业还在收缩。(It's neutral-to-formal register, especially common in financial reporting and news analysis, because it's naturally suited to laying out two opposing facts without passing judgment. A close cousin is "虽然……但是" — although...but — but that one leans more toward a turn or concession. 一方面……另一方面 is more balanced, simply stating two realities that exist at the same time, with neither one overriding the other. This episode's China segment is the clearest example — the stock market's rallying, but manufacturing data is still contracting.)

SAM: 很实用。那第二个词呢？

ALEX: 第二个词是"逆转" — 动词，意思是彻底扭转局面。

SAM: 逆转 — to reverse, to turn completely around.

ALEX: 帕兰泰尔这次的财报就是一次典型的逆转。过去两周股价跌了将近百分之四十，市场都在惩罚资本支出跑得比现金流快的公司；结果一份财报公布，股价直接逆转了整整两周的跌势。(Palantir's earnings this time is a textbook reversal. The stock had fallen almost 40% over the past two weeks, with the market punishing companies whose capex was outrunning their cash flow; then one earnings report came out, and the stock completely reversed two weeks of decline.)

SAM: 逆转跟扭转听起来意思很像，有什么区别吗？

ALEX: 问得好。两个词都有"转变方向"的意思，但逆转更强调彻底、干净地把原来的方向倒过来，常用在比分、市场走势这种有明确方向的东西上，比如逆转比赛、逆转跌势。扭转则更常用在抽象的局面或者印象上，力度也可以比较温和，比如扭转局势、扭转形象，不一定是百分之百倒过来，而是往好的方向调整。今天帕兰泰尔的情况是逆转 — 干净利落，一次财报就把趋势整个翻过来了。(Two words, both meaning "to change direction," but 逆转 emphasizes a complete, clean flip of the original direction — it's often used for scores or market trends, things with a clear direction, like reversing a game or reversing a downtrend. 扭转, by contrast, is more often used for abstract situations or perceptions, and can be gentler in force — turning around a situation, turning around an image — not necessarily a full hundred-percent flip, just an adjustment toward something better. What happened to Palantir today is 逆转 — clean and complete, one earnings report flipped the whole trend.)

SAM: Perfect — one connective for the whole show's tension, one verb for the sharpest single reversal in it. Tagalog naman.

ALEX: Sige. Unang salita natin ngayon: umatras.

SAM: Umatras — to retreat, to back off, to give back ground.

ALEX: Umatras ang piso ngayong araw, mula sa pinakamalakas nitong pagsara sa loob ng mahigit isang buwan noong Lunes. (The peso retreated today, from its strongest close in over a month on Monday.)

SAM: Bakit umatras, gayong ang datos noong Lunes ay maganda naman?

ALEX: Kasi ang pag-angat ng piso noong Lunes ay nakasalalay sa balitang pag-atras umano ng Iran mula sa tensyon sa Hormuz — at ngayong araw, mismong si Tehran ang tumanggi sa balitang iyon. Kaya kung babagsak ang batayan, umaatras din ang lahat ng umasa rito. (Because Monday's rise in the peso was riding on the news that Iran was supposedly backing off from the Hormuz tension — and today, Tehran itself denied that news. So when the foundation gives way, everything that leaned on it retreats too.)

SAM: Ano ang pagkakaiba nito sa bumawi na natutunan na natin dati?

ALEX: Magkasalungat sila sa direksyon — bumawi ay pag-angat pabalik pagkatapos ng pagbagsak, tulad ng ginawa ng piso noong Lunes matapos ang record-low noong Hulyo. Umatras naman ay ang kabaligtaran — pagbibigay-daan, pag-urong pabalik pagkatapos ng isang pag-angat. Kaya ang kwento ng piso sa loob lang ng dalawang araw ay parehong salita — bumawi noong Lunes, umatras naman ngayong Martes. (They're opposite directions — bumawi is rising back up after a fall, like what the peso did on Monday after July's record low. Umatras is the reverse — giving way, pulling back after a rise. So the peso's story across just two days uses both words — it recovered Monday, then retreated Tuesday.)

SAM: Magandang paraan para ilarawan ang buong linggo sa iisang parirala. Pangalawang salita?

ALEX: Lumaho.

SAM: Lumaho — to vanish, to fade away, to dissipate completely.

ALEX: Lumaho na ang bagyong Luis ilang oras lamang matapos tumama sa lupa — mula sa isang malakas na depresyon, naging mababang presyon, at bandang alas-dos ng hapon, lumaho na nang tuluyan. (Storm Luis vanished just hours after making landfall — from a strong depression, to a low-pressure area, and by around two in the afternoon, it had fully dissipated.)

SAM: May kaugnayan din ito sa isang bagay na napag-usapan natin sa Arts segment mamaya, tama?

ALEX: Tama — sa eksibit ni Ellen Pau sa New York, ang tema mismo ay ang paglaho ng Hong Kong na dating kilala niya, unti-unting nawawala habang nagbabago ang lungsod. Kaya lumaho ay hindi lamang para sa bagyo — pwede rin itong ilarawan ang isang bagay na dahan-dahang nawawala, hindi biglaan. (That's right — in Ellen Pau's exhibition in New York, the theme itself is the vanishing of the Hong Kong she used to know, gradually disappearing as the city changes. So lumaho isn't just for storms — it can also describe something slowly fading away, not suddenly.)

SAM: Ano naman ang kaibahan nito sa simpleng mawala?

ALEX: Mas payak at pangkaraniwan ang mawala — basta wala na, kahit anong dahilan. Lumaho naman ay may bahid ng anggulo o kabagalan sa pagkawala — parang usok o hamog na unti-unting kumukupas hanggang sa wala nang bakas. Kaya mas maganda itong gamitin kapag inilalarawan mo ang isang proseso ng pagkawala, hindi lang ang katotohanang wala na. (Mawala is plainer and more everyday — it's simply gone, for whatever reason. Lumaho carries a sense of gradualness in the disappearing — like smoke or mist slowly fading until no trace is left. So it works better when you're describing a process of vanishing, not just the fact that something's gone.)

SAM: Beautiful — a word for the storm that's already gone, and the exhibition we're about to get to, tied together by the same idea of fading rather than disappearing all at once.

SAM: Now to something that's actually splitting opinion right down the middle — Bayreuth.

ALEX: The Bayreuth Festival's 150th-anniversary production, titled "Ring 10010110" — the title's binary code for "150" — has swapped out traditional stage direction for AI-generated live projections, curated by Marcus Lobbes, conducted by Christian Thielemann. And the reception has split sharply along exactly the human-machine line you'd expect.

SAM: How sharply?

ALEX: The Götterdämmerung curtain call drew boos and whistles specifically for Lobbes' production team, while the singers and Thielemann got warm applause. Seen and Heard International's review of the Rheingold night describes a stampede for the doors, unlike anything the critic had seen, and calls the shifting AI imagery — Helmut Kohl, the World Trade Center towers, a reunification stamp — endlessly, pointlessly transforming shapes, disconnected from what's actually being heard.

SAM: That's not a hype piece about AI in opera, that's a real fight over whether it belongs there.

ALEX: Precisely — a hundred and fifty years of interpretive tradition at the most self-consciously important Wagner house in the world, and a real contingent of that audience is telling you generative AI doesn't belong in the room with it.

SAM: And in literature — a genuinely serious piece of criticism worth flagging on its own.

ALEX: T.J. Clark, one of the most important living art critics, has a new essay in the London Review of Books called "Night Visitors," reading the Chauvet cave paintings — bison stampedes, lions, the so-called Venus and Bison-Man pendant, around 37,000 years old — as proof that depiction and language are rival, coexisting cognitive modes, not depiction as some precursor to writing.

SAM: What's his actual argument for that?

ALEX: Language, he argues, is hierarchical, recursive, and conformist by nature. Paleolithic image-making works differently — by reciprocity, overlap, and what he calls excursion, reaching outward rather than folding back on itself the way language does. A serious argument about what pictures are fundamentally for.
