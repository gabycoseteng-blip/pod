## SEGMENT THREE — DEEP DIVE

SAM: Our deep dive today is not about oil or chips, it is about mathematics. The Economist's Today newsletter on Monday led with a piece headlined "The end of mathematics?", alongside a second one, "Top mathematicians are outraged by OpenAI's methods". We have only the newsletter blurbs from The Economist, so the substance here comes from open reporting around the same story.
ALEX: The event itself is unusual. On October 6th, OpenAI posted roughly 720 manuscripts to a public GitHub repository, each claiming progress on one of 372 long-standing open problems. The Economist's phrasing is that no field of research has ever experienced an event like it, and that many mathematicians are not celebrating.
SAM: Counts vary by outlet, 722 papers at first, then 719, and 377 claims in some tallies. The reason the number moved is the best detail in the story. On October 7th, 3 papers were withdrawn, all tied to the Hodge conjecture, which is one of the Millennium Prize problems.
ALEX: The cause was a sign error. The argument assigned a value of plus 1 to reverse traces, expecting a signed count of double points to vanish. But the 2 branches of the standard cusp have opposite source orientations, so each contributes minus 1, and the cancellation never happens. 2 dependent papers fell with the main one. Retraction Watch reported it the next day.
SAM: What does that tell you? A sign error is the most human error imaginable, and it was caught within about 24 hours, in public, by people reading closely.
ALEX: It tells you that the checking worked, but at a cost nobody budgeted for. The repository itself warns that not everything is verified. As of October 7th, only about 42% of the headline results, roughly 300 of the 719, had machine-checked proofs in Lean, the formal proof language. A commenter says none of the withdrawn Hodge papers had been formalized.
SAM: And a Lean check is not a guarantee either, if I understand the objection.
ALEX: Correct, and this is the sharpest critique. A Lean proof tells you the code proves the formal statement. It does not tell you the formal statement says what the paper meant. People are calling this faithfulness. A flawless proof of the wrong theorem is still the wrong theorem. One critic quoted by Retraction Watch said OpenAI should have released the Lean-verified results first and held the rest.
SAM: So the thesis, as the coverage frames it, is that proof generation has become abundant while checking and judging remain scarce.
ALEX: There is even an arXiv title circulating that says it, "Verification abundance, adjudication scarcity". The bottleneck moves from can anyone prove this to who certifies it and who gets the credit.
SAM: Is there a real result in the pile, or is this all volume?
ALEX: There is at least one that held up. A disproof of the Erdős unit-distance conjecture was re-posted in a human-verified version by Noga Alon, Thomas Bloom, Timothy Gowers, Daniel Litt and Will Sawin. That is a serious group putting its names on it. Around the same time, the Erdős problems site froze proof claims, and Terence Tao has been running a multi-part thread on what he calls Math 2.0.
SAM: And the reaction from the field is split.
ALEX: It is. Quanta ran an essay on October 5th asking if AI is the end of math as we know it. Scott Aaronson has written that humans will not be the main theorem-provers. Daniel Litt gave a talk titled The End of Mathematics to about 40 mathematicians at OpenAI's San Francisco office, and per the Washington Post coverage, he thinks the worst case is unlikely but that the field has to adapt now. On the other side, Bruce Schneier and a co-author argue that no, this is not the end of mathematics, at least not yet.
SAM: There are some claims in circulation we should not repeat. One outlet says the dump advanced 3 of the 5 remaining Millennium Problems. That is unconfirmed, and given that the Hodge papers were the ones withdrawn, I would treat it as noise.
ALEX: Right. Also reported but only through secondary sources is a statement from a group calling itself the Association for Human Mathematics urging people to stop working with OpenAI. I would say reported, not established.
SAM: What is actually contested, then?
ALEX: 4 things. First, contamination, whether the models were trained on the very authors' work they are now said to extend. Second, credit and authorship, who is the author of a paper no human wrote. Third, the difference between solved and progress on, which the repository's own wording blurs. And fourth, whether a flood of papers simply overwhelms refereeing, because a referee's hour does not scale the way a GPU does.
SAM: That last one is the second-order effect I find most interesting, because journals and the arXiv will have to set rules. Provenance, formalization requirements, maybe a limit on how many submissions one lab can make.
ALEX: And it travels. The Economist's line is that other disciplines should take heed. Biology, physics, law, anywhere a claim can be generated faster than it can be audited.
SAM: There is a link to the thread we have been pulling all month, who stands behind the number. Here it is who stands behind the proof.
ALEX: Exactly, and for our listener's world it has an echo. A model that produces a thousand interconnection studies a day does not help if the grid operator still has one engineer to review them. Generation is cheap, certification is the bottleneck, and the party that owns certification owns the leverage.
SAM: So the verdict for now: a real acceleration, a real error, caught fast, and a field working out in public who holds the pen.
ALEX: One more sign to watch. If a major journal announces a formalization requirement in the next few weeks, that is the moment the institutions have decided what counts as a proof in the age of abundance.

## SEGMENT SEVEN — MANDARIN VOCAB

SAM: Today's 2 Mandarin words both come from China's trade story. The first is 顺差, which means a surplus, and almost always a trade surplus.
ALEX: It is a formal, written-register word, the language of customs data and finance desks. The standard collocation is 贸易顺差, a trade surplus, and the verb that goes with it is 出现 or 保持, to record or to maintain one. Its opposite is 逆差, a deficit. The near-synonym is 盈余, which is a surplus in the broader sense, like a budget or an account, while 顺差 is specifically about the balance between what flows out and what flows in.
SAM: Here is an example. 今年前八个月，中国的贸易顺差已经超过8000亿美元。(In the first 8 months of this year, China's trade surplus has already exceeded $800 billion.)
ALEX: The second word is 取而代之, and this is the abstract one, a 4-character expression that works like a connective. It means to take the place of something and become the replacement. It is formal, 书面语, and it nearly always carries a before-and-after story: something fades, and something else takes its place.
SAM: What is the structure?
ALEX: Usually the thing that fades comes first, then 取而代之 in a new clause. Typically with 的是 in front: 取而代之的是, which means what replaced it was. Contrast it with 代替, which is neutral and also works for ordinary substitutions like replacing a part. 取而代之 is heavier, it describes a change in who holds the position.
SAM: Example, please. 房地产不再是增长的引擎，取而代之的是出口。(Real estate is no longer the engine of growth; what has taken its place is exports.)
ALEX: And one more for 顺差, with a contrast. 出口增长很快，国内需求却很弱，所以顺差越来越大。(Exports are growing fast but domestic demand is weak, so the surplus keeps getting bigger.)
SAM: Both of those are going to come back in the China segment, right now.

ALEX: The steelman is that a bank that waits for the bond market to calm down is outsourcing its judgment, and when yields rise because inflation expectations drift, doing nothing is itself a policy choice.
SAM: The counter-take is the opposite risk. A central bank that moves because yields moved is letting the bond market set policy. And when yields are high because of fiscal supply or war premia, tightening can make the problem worse. The Philippines is a live example, where the BSP is being urged to hike into a peso slide that is partly imported oil.
ALEX: Which is also the answer to the column. The inertia charge is fair when expectations are drifting. It is unfair when the yield is a bill for something a rate hike cannot fix.
