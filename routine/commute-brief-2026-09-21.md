## CHINA (1 item, debt-deflation lens)
- china-presale-overhaul-goldman-30pct-land-revenue-hit: Goldman Sachs (Sept 7) revised its estimate of Beijing's presale-to-completed-home-sales shift: now projects a 30% cut to land-sale revenue, up from an earlier 20% estimate. Mechanism: requiring completed (not presale) units makes it harder for cash-strapped developers to bid for new land — a policy-driven, not just demand-driven, hit. Local governments have historically drawn ~40% of revenue from land sales; local government debt is estimated near $18.9 trillion, roughly matching China's GDP. Second-order read: converts a liquidity/confidence problem (developers can't sell) into a structural fiscal problem (the local-government revenue base itself shrinks by policy design) — even as Beijing expands central borrowing to backstop local governments, the land-revenue base underneath keeps eroding by design. New angle vs. the already-covered auction-fizzle/price-collapse stories.

## OP-EDS & COMMENTARY (2)
1. Jonathan Levin, Bloomberg Opinion, "The Policy Muddle Snaring the World's Big Central Banks" (Sept 20). Argument: the US, UK and Japan simultaneously talk tough on inflation while quietly leaning against rising long-bond yields (buybacks, dovish signaling, jawboning) — accelerator and brake at once — none taking the politically hard step of shrinking deficits.
