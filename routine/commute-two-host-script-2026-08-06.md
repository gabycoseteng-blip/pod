## SEGMENT — DEEP DIVE

SAM: Alex, you flagged something out of SemiAnalysis this week that I think ties everything else we've talked about together — the efficiency question underneath the AI trade.

ALEX: This is genuinely one of the more interesting technical pieces I've read in a while, and it's about a Chinese model, Kimi K3, out of a lab called Moonshot AI. The piece is titled, half-jokingly, "Kimi K3, The Manos, The Mythos, The Legendos" — but underneath the joke title is a serious teardown of the model's architecture, specifically something called Kimi Delta Attention, or KDA.

SAM: Walk me through what that actually does, at the level we'd care about, not the math.

ALEX: As your context gets longer, the memory you need to hold — the KV cache — grows fast, and that's a huge chunk of your actual inference cost. Most efficient designs try to replace expensive full attention entirely with a cheaper, linear kind. Kimi K3 doesn't — it uses a 3-to-1 ratio, three layers of cheap linear attention for every one layer of full, expensive attention.

SAM: So it's a hybrid, not a full replacement.

ALEX: Right, and that's what SemiAnalysis zeroes in on. Even one full-attention layer every four cuts KV-cache and memory use by up to about 75% on long sequences, while preserving the recall pure linear attention tends to lose. Their line: the 3-to-1 ratio is "a confession and an achievement at the same time" — an achievement because it's a real efficiency win, a confession because linear attention alone still can't fully replace the expensive kind.

SAM: Why does that matter to someone thinking about power demand, not just AI research?

ALEX: It's a second lever on the electrons-wall question — do efficiency gains blunt total power demand, or just make compute cheaper per unit and pull in more total usage, Jevons paradox in real time. We've talked about hardware efficiency, chips doing more work per megawatt; this is the same tension one layer up, in the software itself. Cheaper inference eases pressure on data-center demand — but it can just as easily accelerate the buildout, because more applications become economically viable to run at scale.

SAM: And notable that it's a Chinese lab pulling this off.

ALEX: That's the headline underneath the headline — Kimi K3 swept several open-model leaderboards on release, a clear signal the gap between the Chinese open-source frontier and the closed US labs has essentially closed on raw capability. The competitive story isn't just who has the most GPUs anymore, it's who squeezes the most useful work out of the GPUs they have.

SAM: Dense piece, worth chewing on rather than skimming.

ALEX: Attribution where it's due — SemiAnalysis, from their newsletter this week, for anyone who wants the full technical breakdown.

SAM: And speaking of data-center buildout running into limits — Texas just did something pretty dramatic.

ALEX: Power demand, water use, any public financial assistance the project's received, community protections, and — this is the interesting one — ownership. Abbott's directive specifically wants to know who actually owns each project in the queue.

ALEX: There's a live worry across ERCOT and PJM alike about opaque or foreign capital behind speculative interconnection requests — placeholders with no real intention to build. ALEX: Because capital's now pricing distributed residential batteries as genuine grid-capacity infrastructure, not just backup power. Base claims 500-plus megawatt-hours deployed and 200-plus megawatts in utility deals — aggregated at scale, a real alternative to the gas-turbine capacity utilities are racing to build for data-center load. The Austin-manufactured angle also plays into the tariff story around Chinese LFP cells we flagged with the Lazard cost data — a distinct, fresh capital story on top of that trend.

SAM: So today's energy story in one line: Texas hits the brakes on the supply side, PJM utilities race to lock in demand before the auction prices it for them, and capital keeps hedging toward distributed alternatives to both.

## SEGMENT FOUR — VOCAB OF THE DAY

SAM: Time for today's vocab. Two Mandarin, then two Tagalog, and as always, we're doing this one in the language itself.

ALEX: 我们先说普通话。今天的第一个词是"归根结底。" (Let's start with Mandarin. Today's first word is "guī gēn jié dǐ.")

SAM: 归根结底是什么意思呢？(What does guī gēn jié dǐ mean?)

ALEX: 归根结底的意思是"说到底"或者"从根本上说"——用来引出一件事情最核心、最本质的结论。这是比较正式的书面语，常见于财经评论和时事分析。(Guī gēn jié dǐ means "at the end of the day" or "when you get down to the root of it" — it's used to introduce the most fundamental conclusion about something. It's a fairly formal, written-register expression, common in financial commentary and current-affairs analysis.)

SAM: 能不能用今天的深度报道举个例子？(Can you give an example using today's deep-dive story?)

ALEX: 当然可以。比如说："归根结底，Kimi K3的效率提升到底是会减少电力需求，还是会刺激更多算力涌入，现在还不好说。" (Of course. For example: "At the end of the day, whether Kimi K3's efficiency gain will actually reduce power demand, or just draw in even more compute, is still hard to say.")

SAM: 归根结底跟"总的来说"有什么区别？(What's the difference between guī gēn jié dǐ and "zǒng de lái shuō," generally speaking?)

ALEX: "总的来说"只是笼统总结，语气随意；"归根结底"带有分析的力度，暗示这才是真正的核心。财经报道里更常用后者。("Zǒng de lái shuō" is a loose, casual summary. "Guī gēn jié dǐ" carries more analytical weight — this is the real core underneath. Financial reporting reaches for the latter more often.)

SAM: 明白了。第二个词是什么？(Got it. What's the second word?)

ALEX: 第二个词是"息息相关。" (The second word is "xī xī xiāng guān.")

SAM: 这个词听起来是个成语。(That sounds like a four-character idiom.)

ALEX: 没错，意思是"紧密相连、彼此影响"，通常用在两件事看起来不同、但实际上互相牵连很深的时候。(Correct — it means "closely, intimately connected," and it's usually used when two things look unrelated on the surface but are actually deeply intertwined.)

SAM: 今天的新闻里能找到例子吗？(Can we find an example in today's news?)

ALEX: 当然。比如："霍尔木兹海峡的过路费谈判，跟全球油价的走势息息相关。" (Of course. For example: "The Hormuz Strait toll-fee negotiations are closely tied to the direction of global oil prices.") 再比如："德州暂停数据中心并网审批，跟整个电网的稳定息息相关。" (Or another example: "Texas pausing data-center interconnection approvals is closely tied to the stability of the whole grid.")

SAM: 息息相关跟普通的"有关"有什么不一样？(How is xī xī xiāng guān different from the plain word "related to"?)

ALEX: "有关"比较口语化模糊，只说存在某种联系；"息息相关"程度更强，强调密不可分，一方变化另一方必受影响。("Yǒuguān" is casual and vague. "Xī xī xiāng guān" is stronger — it stresses the two are inseparable; if one changes, the other is guaranteed to be affected.)

SAM: Now let's switch to Tagalog. Ngayon, sa Tagalog naman tayo.

ALEX: Oo. Ang unang salita natin ngayon ay "humupa." (Yes. Our first word today is "humupa.")

SAM: Ano ang ibig sabihin ng humupa? (What does humupa mean?)

ALEX: Ang humupa ay ibig sabihing bumababa o unti-unting nawawala ang lakas ng isang bagay — bagyo, sakit, o kahit galit. (Humupa means to subside, or for the strength of something — a storm, an illness, even anger — to gradually fade away.)

SAM: Puwede bang gamitin natin ito sa balitang pangkapaligiran ngayon? (Can we use this in today's weather story?)

ALEX: Oo naman. Halimbawa: "Humupa na ang bagyong Maymay pagkalabas nito sa Northern Luzon, mula bagyo tungong depresyon tropikal." (For example: "Storm Maymay subsided after it exited Northern Luzon, weakening from a tropical storm into a tropical depression.")

SAM: Ano ang pagkakaiba ng humupa sa humina, na natutunan na natin dati? (What's the difference between humupa and humina, which we already learned before?)

ALEX: Ang humina ay pangkalahatan — bumaba lang ang lakas, kahit anong bagay. Pero ang humupa ay mas espesipiko — unti-unting nawawala hanggang halos wala na, tulad ng baha o bagyo na humuhupa. (Humina is general — strength just decreased. Humupa is more specific — gradually receding until almost gone, like floodwater or a storm subsiding.)

SAM: Malinaw na. Ano ang ikalawang salita? (Clear. What's the second word?)

ALEX: Ang ikalawang salita ay "isulong." (The second word is "isulong.")

SAM: Isulong — parang may kinalaman ito sa pagpapatuloy ng isang plano? (Isulong — does this have to do with pushing a plan forward?)

ALEX: Tama ka. Ang isulong ay ibig sabihing itaguyod o ipagpatuloy nang may sigla ang isang adyenda, panukala, o layunin. (You're right. Isulong means to advance or push forward — with real energy — an agenda, a proposal, or a goal.)

SAM: Gamitin natin ito sa balitang pampulitika ngayon. (Let's use it in today's political story.)

ALEX: Sige. Halimbawa: "Isinulong ni Pangulong Marcos ang dalawampu't apat na priyoridad na panukalang batas sa ikaapat na pagpupulong ng LEDAC." (Sure. For example: "President Marcos pushed forward twenty-four priority bills at the fourth LEDAC meeting.")

SAM: May pagkakaiba ba ito sa salitang "itaguyod"? (Is this different from the word "itaguyod," to champion or support?)

ALEX: Magkatulad, pero ang itaguyod ay tungkol sa pagsuporta habang hinihimok ito ng iba. Ang isulong ay may diin sa personal na paghila nito pasulong. Kaya sa LEDAC, mas angkop ang isulong — si Marcos mismo ang aktibong nagpapatakbo nito. (Similar, but itaguyod is about supporting a proposal as others advance it. Isulong emphasizes personally pulling it forward. So for LEDAC, isulong fits better — Marcos himself is actively driving it.)

SAM: Perfect — 归根结底, 息息相关, humupa, isulong. Four solid words for today.
