## DEEP DIVE — path: INBOX (Industrial Base Alpha newsletter)
GE Aerospace buys Consolidated Precision Products for $11.75bn (Sep 14, read in full via Gmail). GE is spending ~75% of cash on hand (~26x 2027E EBITDA / ~18x with synergies) to acquire its own 15-year castings supplier from PE owners Warburg Pincus/Berkshire Partners — because no supply contract can guarantee capacity when LEAP/GEnx airfoil demand is forecast to grow 30%+ by 2030. Castings are metallurgy-as-craft (trailing edges fractions of a millimeter thick), alloy-constrained, COVID-hollowed-out on skilled labor (5-year retrain lag); every part/process/supplier combo needs 18-36 months of OEM requalification. GE decided owning the bottleneck beats waiting in line for it. Cross-domain parallel for an infra audience: same logic as an interconnection queue or transformer allocation — a physical, requalification-gated bottleneck a cash-rich buyer chooses to vertically integrate rather than contract around. ## ENERGY / DATA CENTERS — path: INBOX (CTVC + Dealflow.energy newsletters)
DOE's Loan Programs Office rebrands as "Office of Energy Dominance Financing" (EDF); first 2026-2030 strategic plan published (CTVC, Sep 14, read in full).
- Two lending doors: Section 1703 (tech-too-new-for-banks, $40bn IRA top-up) **expires Sept 30** — its loss-reserve subsidy rescinded, so applicants must now self-fund the risk cushion in cash before a conditional commitment. Section 1706 (repower/retool existing infra, $250bn) extended to **2028** via OBBBA with a $1bn credit-subsidy backstop.
- EDF has already restructured/killed **$83bn** of Biden-era book: $29.9bn deobligated outright ($9.5bn of it wind/solar) — including cutting Ford's BlueOval SK loan from $9.6bn to $3.8bn after the JV dissolved.
- Money flowing now: **$31.4bn to utilities**, led by **$26.5bn to Southern Company** (Georgia/Alabama) — the largest single package in DOE history; **$17.8bn in conditional commitments**, nearly all ($17.5bn) for Westinghouse AP1000 nuclear components; $489m to Pattern Energy for a 220MW Puerto Rico battery project (solar component + emissions-control condition both dropped from the original Biden-era deal).
- Structural shift: the fossil-generation emissions-mitigation requirement on 1706 projects is gone; eligibility widened to coal/gas repowering, pipeline replacement, refinery retrofits, minerals processing.
- Practitioner read: the LPO's old value was signaling — a conditional commitment told private lenders/offtakers diligence was done, clearing first-of-a-kind projects through the "valley of death." EDF now underwrites *low-risk* capacity add-ons for already-creditworthy regulated utilities (rate-recovery-backed), not novel-tech derisking — financing anything unproven (hydrogen, offshore wind, non-nuclear novel gen) just got materially harder.
- Bonus (Dealflow.energy, same week): Hut8/Silver Basin Digital Infrastructure has a live Texas PUCT docket to net-meter a **180MW data center** behind an existing 204MWac solar farm (Taygete II, Pecos County) — Commission Staff recommended approval Sept 8, TNMP filed adverse testimony. ## OP-EDS & COMMENTARY
1. opeds-ramos-amodei-ai-pause — Gabriela Ramos, Project Syndicate (Sept 14), "What Amodei's Call for an AI Pause Gets Wrong." Steelman: Amodei's Sept 12 call to deliberately pace frontier AI development has real logic — capability is outrunning developers' ability to reliably control behavior, and industry-led slowdown buys time for safety research before a catastrophic release. Counter: self-interested corporate pacing isn't governance — a single firm slowing itself doesn't bind rivals (especially in China), and it lets governments keep abdicating the job of building binding public oversight. [directly follows the Amodei/Trump headline]
2. opeds-wapo-trump-putin-unwinnable-wars — Washington Post Opinion (Sept 12), "Trump and Putin are both wrong: they can't win their wars." Steelman: both leaders pursue military/diplomatic goals — Trump's Iran confrontation, Putin's Ukraine grind — that battlefield/negotiating realities won't deliver; both keep rejecting bad news instead of adjusting course. Counter: the parallel risks false equivalence — flattening a democracy defending itself against invasion (Ukraine) and a dictatorship's war of aggression into symmetrical "leader hubris" understates the moral asymmetry.
