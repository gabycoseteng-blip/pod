## 6. DEEP DIVE — source: INBOX (SemiAnalysis, path A)
semianalysis-spacex-10gw-2027-microsoft-offtake: SemiAnalysis, "SpaceX 10GW in 2027 – Why It's Real, Will Drive $500B ARR for SpaceX, and Why Microsoft Will Be the Largest Offtaker" (received Aug 7). On SpaceX's first public earnings call, Musk guided to 6-8GW incremental compute in 2027 alone, "potential well above 10GW." At SemiAnalysis's ~$50B/GW cost assumption, that's $300-500B of 2027 capex — comparable to AWS's or Google's full-year capex, from a far-less-profitable company. The economics: running frontier models on a GB300 cluster can generate >$100B/GW/year in API inference revenue against ~$12B/GW/year cost — Anthropic's Opus reportedly runs 85%+ gross margins. Microsoft's April 2026 reworked OpenAI deal dropped the old 20% revenue-share, so Microsoft now has full inference upside — it's already signed >10GW of binding capacity year-to-date (~$300B in new commitments), a sharp reversal of its prior leasing pause; the stated bet is Azure revenue growth jumping from ~42% to 100%+ next year by shifting mix from cheap IaaS ($14M/MW/year serving OpenAI) toward high-margin Foundry/Copilot. SpaceX's financing puzzle (no hyperscaler balance sheet): Nvidia vendor financing (explains Musk's on-call "Nvidia exclusive" declaration, abandoning prior TPU/AMD evaluation) plus premium $30-50M/MW/year pricing justified by 3-5 month build times. Build-speed evidence: Colossus 1 (300MW) built in 122 days; an onsite gas plant built 1km across the Mexico border to dodge US permitting; Southaven, MS scaled from ~495MW (Feb 2026) to >1.2GW (July). Current SpaceX capacity only ~2GW by end of 2026 — 10GW in 2027 means a 5x acceleration in one year, almost entirely on onsite gas. Discussion hook: is this Nvidia-financing-the-buyer-of-its-own-chips (echoing the OpenAI circular-financing story already aired), or a genuinely different, demand-validated bet given the token economics?
