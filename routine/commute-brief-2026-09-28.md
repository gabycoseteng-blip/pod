## ENERGY / DATA CENTERS / UTILITIES (source: INBOX — Utility Dive Daily Dive)
1. FERC rejects Oklo's complaint against PJM (decided Sept 24, reported Utility Dive 9/25 Daily Dive).
   Oklo's 750MW Virginia project (150MW advanced nuclear "Aurora" + 300MW fuel cells + 300MW gas) was
   dropped by PJM from its current interconnection study cycle in early August over an unresolved inverter
   ride-through test flaw. Oklo filed a FERC complaint (Docket EL26-101) to force reinstatement; FERC
   ruled PJM broke no rules and Oklo never cured the flagged deficiency. Oklo's options: fix the
   application and wait for PJM's next full study cycle, or file into PJM's Expedited Interconnection
   Track (open through December). Shows SMR/advanced-nuclear projects don't get a queue pass just for
   being "firm"/AI-adjacent — a technical-compliance bottleneck, distinct from capacity-shortage stories
   already aired.
2. Bloomberg (Sept 25, via Utility Dive): AI's nuclear power push hitting cost/regulatory walls despite
   capital flooding in. US nuclear startups raised $4.6B YTD 2026 (vs $3.8B all of 2025). But only TWO new
   commercial reactors are under construction in the US/Canada; no other new commercial projects cleared
   regulatory approval; Bloomberg Intelligence doesn't see widespread SMR commercialization until ~2035.
   Quote: "the nuclear renaissance is currently still showing up more on balance sheets than construction
   sites."
SECOND-ORDER READ: pair these two — money is chasing advanced nuclear as the "firm, clean, always-on"
answer to hyperscaler load, but the gating factor isn't fundraising, it's project-by-project regulatory/
interconnection execution (FERC/RTO technical compliance, NRC licensing, supply chain). For a listener
underwriting or scheduling data-center power deals: an "SMR PPA signed" doesn't de-risk your
interconnection timeline — the queue/technical review is a separate gate that can move COD by a full
study cycle regardless of capital or offtake behind the project.

## OP-EDS & COMMENTARY (1-2, need counter-take)
1. Abby McCloskey, Bloomberg Opinion, Sept 25 2026 — "Vance's Plan for Stay-at-Home Moms Thinks Too Small."
   Steelman: The White House plan would let married, stay-at-home parents draw payments from the Child
   Care and Development Block Grant (CCDBG) — a fund built for low-income WORKING parents' daycare costs.
   McCloskey argues the goal (support for at-home caregiving) is right but the mechanism too small/timid;
   she'd rather see a bigger fix like an expanded child tax credit.
   Counter-take: this isn't just "too small" — it's zero-sum and regressive. CCDBG is a fixed, already-
   underfunded pot; diverting it to pay one-earner married households necessarily shrinks subsidized
   daycare slots for lower-income single parents who need that care specifically to be able to work. It
   rewards a family-structure choice by degrading the work-enabling subsidy for the parents who most need
   to work. McCloskey's "go bigger" critique elides this distributional problem.
2. John Authers, Bloomberg Opinion, Sept 25 2026 — "The Big One Is Rumbling in the Bond Market."
   Steelman: global long-government-bond yields have climbed to multi-decade highs (ties directly to the
   US 10Y hitting its highest since June 2007 this week); a trend visible since the post-pandemic
   inflation/rate cycle has suddenly accelerated — a "tectonic" repricing of the long end, the "keystone of
   the entire financial system." Not yet a crisis, but a warning tremor.
   Counter-take: this reads like regime change but may just be term-premium normalization — post-2020
   yields were artificially suppressed by QE and pandemic-era demand for duration; a reversion to
   historical term premia as central banks normalize balance sheets doesn't require a "tectonic" story,
   just an unwind of an anomaly. The "keystone" framing risks treating a slow, well-telegraphed structural
   shift as sudden fragility.
