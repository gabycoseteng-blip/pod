## SEGMENT — DEEP DIVE
ALEX: Let's go deep on one thing today — this is the kind of piece we build a whole segment around. SemiAnalysis spent six months trying to reverse-engineer something PJM has never fully explained: the model behind its "Reserve Requirement Study," the black box that decides how much capacity PJM has to buy at auction every year.
SAM: They found two very specific, very boring-sounding errors that turn out to be worth billions. First — PJM's model gives gas turbines zero credit for the fact that they produce more power in cold weather. Their own class tables show an average uprate of 8.4% in winter, some units up to 25% more output when it's cold — none of that gets counted.
ALEX: Second — since the 2022 Winter Storm Elliott, roughly 400 of PJM's 750 thermal units have actually filed winterization upgrades. But the model is still weighting 2022's failure data at full force, as if none of that hardening happened.
SAM: Put those two corrections together and PJM is undercrediting its own existing fleet by somewhere between 3.1 and 4.7 gigawatts of real, already-built, already-reliable capacity.
ALEX: Here's why that number matters so much. PJM's supply curve isn't a smooth line — about 79% of all bids come in cheap, 2 to 5 dollars a megawatt-day. Then it goes essentially vertical near the requirement, up to 270, 350 dollars. A tiny correction near that kink swings the entire clearing price.
SAM: SemiAnalysis ran the counterfactual. Correcting the model would have cleared the 2025-26 auction 6.7 billion dollars cheaper — and that only required 14 fewer megawatts of procurement to trigger. The 2026-27 auction would've cleared 4.9 billion cheaper. Combined, 11.6 billion dollars, out of 63.6 billion paid across the last four auctions.
ALEX: It's not backward-looking trivia either — it directly sets up the emergency backstop auction running September 30th through October 21st, results out December 2nd. PJM's model says there's a 6.8 gigawatt shortfall to cover. SemiAnalysis's corrected model says it's closer to 3 gigawatts.
SAM: Which makes the governance fight underneath this even more infuriating. PJM's own task force actually passed a winterization-accreditation fix back in July of last year, 178 to 54, 77% support. A senior stakeholder committee killed it 26 days later, with only 30.7% support.
ALEX: Then this past July, PJM's board rejected a different fix — a voluntary-subscription design for the backstop auction that two-thirds of stakeholders, sector-weighted, had actually endorsed — in favor of a 555-dollar-per-megawatt-day price cap, pay-as-bid structure, with no committed counterparty on the other side. Contracts on that run all the way to 2043.
SAM: Load-serving entities that participate have to post 1.5 million dollars per megawatt in credit support within 90 days. At that price cap on a 6.8 gigawatt shortfall, the maximum liability is something like 21 billion dollars.
ALEX: One more number worth sitting with — existing generators themselves are bidding into these auctions at 8 to 14 dollars a megawatt-day, per PJM's own independent market monitor. They're clearing 20 to 40 times higher than that. The median existing combined-cycle plant already made 407% of its going-forward costs from energy and ancillary markets alone last year, before a single dollar of capacity payment.
SAM: FERC's comment deadline on PJM's backstop filing is August 21st, three days from today. This is a live fight, not a postmortem — a feature in SemiAnalysis, and full credit to their team for doing the reverse-engineering nobody else bothered to do.
ALEX: And it's worth being precise about what kind of error this is, because it's not fraud and it's not even really incompetence — it's a model built on conservative, backward-looking assumptions that never got updated as the underlying fleet actually got hardened. Nobody had an incentive to go back and re-check it, because a shortage that clears at a high price doesn't look like a bug from the inside. It looks like the market working.
SAM: Which is exactly why it took an outside team spending six months on a black box to find it, instead of PJM's own stakeholder process, which as we just heard, had two separate chances to fix pieces of this and voted both of them down.

Fermi Incorporated, the nuclear-powered AI data-center campus in Amarillo, Texas that former Energy Secretary Rick Perry co-founded, just hired Lee McIntire as CEO — forty years at Bechtel, CH2M Hill, TerraPower, McDermott, a career spent actually building megaprojects, not financing them. ## SEGMENT FOUR — VOCAB OF THE DAY
ALEX: Time for vocab of the day. Two Mandarin words, then two Tagalog, both tied to stories we just covered.
SAM: 好，我们先说普通话。(Okay, let's start with Mandarin.)
ALEX: 第一个词是"换句话说"。SemiAnalysis算出，PJM的模型少算了大约四十亿瓦的现有产能——换句话说，只要修正这四十亿瓦的误差，两年的拍卖价格本可以便宜一百一十六亿美元。(In other words, correcting that four-gigawatt error alone would have made two years of auction prices $11.6 billion cheaper.)
SAM: "换句话说"这个说法书面语和口语都能用，分析师解释复杂问题的时候特别常用。它跟"也就是说"意思很接近，但"也就是说"更随口一点；"换句话说"更像是刻意换一种说法，让专业内容变得容易懂。(“换句话说” works in both written and spoken Mandarin, and analysts use it constantly to explain something complicated. It's close to “也就是说,” but that one is more casual — “换句话说” signals a deliberate rephrase to make something technical more accessible.)
ALEX: 第二个词是"潜在"，意思是potential。霍尔木兹海峡的协议一旦到期，市场立刻重新计入这个潜在风险——原油涨到每桶90.71美元，三十年期美债收益率升到5.31%，创2007年6月以来新高。(The moment the Hormuz truce lapsed, the market immediately re-priced this potential risk — oil rose to $90.71 a barrel, and the 30-year yield hit 5.31%, its highest since June 2007.)
SAM: "潜在"是书面语，金融和风险相关的文章里到处都是——潜在风险、潜在影响、潜在客户。跟"现实"正好相反："潜在"的风险还没发生，只是被市场提前计入价格了；"现实"的风险已经发生了。(“潜在” is formal, and shows up everywhere in finance and risk writing — potential risk, potential impact, potential client. It's the opposite of “现实,” actual or realized — a potential risk hasn't happened yet, the market's just pricing it in ahead of time; a realized risk already has.)
ALEX: Now let's switch to Tagalog. Ngayon, sa Tagalog naman tayo.
SAM: Una, ang salitang "pangungurakot." Sinabi ni dating DPWH Sekretaryong Bonoan sa Sandiganbayan na umano'y dumanas ng malawakang pangungurakot ang 24-bilyong-pisong "leadership fund" ng Senado. (Former DPWH Secretary Bonoan told the Sandiganbayan that the Senate's 24-billion-peso "leadership fund" allegedly suffered widespread plunder.)
ALEX: Mas malakas at mas pormal ang "pangungurakot" kumpara sa payak na katiwalian — partikular itong tumutukoy sa malawakan, sistematikong pagnanakaw ng pondo ng bayan, kaya madalas itong ginagamit sa mga plunder case sa Sandiganbayan, kasama ang mga salitang pondo at kaban ng bayan. (“Pangungurakot” is stronger and more formal than plain corruption — it specifically means large-scale, systemic theft of public funds, which is why it shows up in plunder cases before the Sandiganbayan, alongside words like "funds" and "the public coffers.")
SAM: Pangalawa, ang salitang "pagtanggi." Sa kabila ng testimonya ni Bonoan, mariing ipinahayag ni dating Senate President Zubiri ang kanyang pagtanggi sa alegasyon. (Despite Bonoan's testimony, former Senate President Zubiri firmly issued his denial of the allegation.)
ALEX: Pormal itong salita, karaniwang ginagamit sa balita kapag opisyal na tumatanggi sa akusasyon — mas mabigat ito kaysa sa payak na "ayaw" sa pang-araw-araw na usapan. Kabaligtaran ito ng pag-amin. ("Pagtanggi" is formal, the word news reports use when someone official denies an accusation — heavier than the everyday "ayaw." It's the opposite of pag-amin, admission.)
SAM: That's vocab for today — 换句话说, 潜在, pangungurakot, and pagtanggi.
