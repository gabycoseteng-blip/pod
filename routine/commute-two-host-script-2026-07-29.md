## SEGMENT TWO — ENERGY / DATA CENTERS / UTILITIES
ALEX: Today's lead is out of Texas, and it's the clearest evidence yet of just how much load is trying to jump the interconnection queue.
SAM: Give me the number.
ALEX: CenterPoint has submitted 17 gigawatts of large-load projects into Texas's new "Batch Zero" interconnection process, with roughly 14 gigawatts expected to be eligible — 10 gigawatts of base-load already fully studied, 4 gigawatts of studied-load with one of two required studies complete. There's about 900 million dollars in customer cash commitments and deposits backing the signed facility-extension agreements. CEO Jason Wells said on this week's earnings call that nearly all of it gets energized by the end of 2030 — which implies roughly 50% growth in Houston-area peak load by the end of 2029, off a current peak of 21 gigawatts.
SAM: That's the load side. What's the demand model saying now?
ALEX: BNEF just shifted methodology on this — instead of tracking noisy announced-megawatt figures, which get revised constantly, they're now forecasting bottoms-up off actual chip shipments. New base case: 118 gigawatts of US data center capacity by 2030, 207 gigawatts by 2033. That's a meaningfully more credible number because it's anchored to something you can actually count — chips shipping — rather than press releases.
SAM: And on the supply side, there's real friction in PJM this week.
ALEX: PJM's board proposed a one-time backstop capacity auction starting in September, capped at 555 dollars per megawatt-day, specifically to fill the 6.8 gigawatt shortfall left over from the 2028-29 base auction — which, remember, already cleared at its own cap of 325. Paired with that: new large loads that don't bring their own generation would get curtailed near emergency grid conditions. That filing goes to FERC before the end of July.
SAM: Walk me through why that backstop cap number actually matters, beyond just being a bigger number.
ALEX: This is the sharpest data point in the whole segment. 555 is 71% above the 325 the base auction just cleared at — and crucially, the backstop only buys curtailable, last-resort capacity, not firm power. So you now have a market-priced number for what unfirm reliability actually costs at the margin. And it lines up almost perfectly with something LBNL just published — their analysis says nationally, inflation-adjusted retail power prices are only 3% above 2019, which pushes back hard on the popular "data centers are spiking your bill" narrative. But in constrained regions — and they name PJM specifically — growth forces urgent, high-cost buildouts that clear expensive auctions before cost-allocation even catches up. The 555 number is the proof: that's what the market is charging for reliability it can't otherwise firm up in time.
SAM: So the national story and the PJM story are actually two different stories wearing the same headline.
ALEX: Exactly right. Nationally, in real terms, power prices are roughly flat since 2019. But LBNL's regional breakdown shows California up 6 cents a kilowatt-hour and the Northeast up 2 to 4 cents since 2019, while much of the middle of the country is flat to down. The old rule was that load growth lowers unit prices, because you spread fixed costs over more megawatt-hours. In constrained zones, that inverts — growth forces the expensive buildout first, and the bill lands before the benefit does.
SAM: A few more before we move — New York and Maryland both have relevant developments.
ALEX: New York's data-center moratorium is currently masking stalled legislation that would force large data centers to source at least a third of their power from renewables by 2030 — and that provision could reach existing facilities, not just new ones, especially water-intensive cooling sites, the moment the moratorium lifts. And in Maryland, Pepco's rate case just got sharper — the state's consumer advocate is now arguing for an outright cut to Pepco's allowed return on equity, not just a smaller increase than requested. That's a live test of whether affordability backlash can actually compress utility returns, not just slow their growth.
SAM: On the cost side, storage got more expensive too.
ALEX: Lazard's latest storage cost benchmark has standalone four-hour battery systems back up to 210 to 292 dollars per megawatt-hour, reversing two years of steady declines — driven by tariffs on Chinese LFP battery cells cutting off the cheap-cell supply chain that had been driving costs down. And DOE's draft national transmission needs study put a number on grid congestion itself: 12 billion dollars in wholesale power costs in 2024, up from 11 billion in 2023, concentrated in roughly 5% of hours — the cold snaps and high-net-load moments. The biggest relief potential sits in NYISO, NorthernGrid South, and MISO, with inter-regional transmission ties flagged as the single highest-value fix.
SAM: Texas submitting 17 gigawatts at once, a backstop auction pricing what unfirm power actually costs, and battery costs reversing course. A dense one today.
ALEX: One more thing worth flagging before we move — tonight's Microsoft and Meta prints aren't just an AI-capex story, they're a power story too. Every dollar of that capex guide has a megawatt figure attached to it somewhere in a filing, and after today's PJM and CenterPoint numbers, the market's going to be reading those capex lines with a much sharper eye on where the power is actually coming from, not just how much is being spent.
SAM: So the energy segment and tonight's earnings are really the same story from two different angles.
ALEX: Exactly — capex without a firm power source behind it is just a spending number. The physical build keeps accelerating faster than the market structure meant to price it. On to the Philippines.

## SEGMENT FOUR — VOCAB OF THE DAY
SAM: 到了学中文的时间了。
ALEX: 没错。今天两个词都跟今天的新闻直接相关，我们用中文聊。
SAM: 好，你先来。
ALEX: 第一个词是"分歧"，名词，书面语，今天美联储的新闻里正好能用上。
SAM: 怎么用？
ALEX: 今天联储内部很可能出现分歧——达拉斯联储的Logan和克利夫兰联储的Hammack预计都会投反对票，这是沃什主席上任以来第一次出现这种分歧。
SAM: "分歧"跟"分裂"有什么不一样？
ALEX: 区别很关键。"分裂"是真正的破裂，意味着这个机构可能因此分开、无法正常运作。"分歧"程度轻得多，只是内部意见不一致，但整个机构照常运转，决定照样能做出来。今天联储的情况就是分歧，不是分裂——两位委员投反对票，但利率决定还是会正常公布。
SAM: 明白了，分歧是"意见不合"，分裂是"真的散了"。
ALEX: 对，搭配上也要注意，我们常说"出现分歧"，"内部分歧"，都是固定搭配。
SAM: 我来说第二个词："未必"，这个可以用在今天Visa的新闻上。
ALEX: 说说看。
SAM: Visa这次业绩全面超预期，但是股价盘前还是跌了——这就说明，达标未必意味着上涨。
ALEX: "未必"跟口语里常说的"不一定"有什么区别？
SAM: 意思差不多，但是"未必"更正式，更书面，常常出现在财经评论或者严肃的分析文章里；"不一定"更口语化，日常聊天用得多。而且"未必"这个词本身带一点委婉的反驳语气——不是直接否定，而是说"这不一定成立"，留有余地。
ALEX: 所以今天晚上微软和Meta公布财报的时候，我们也可以说，业绩超预期未必能让股价上涨。
SAM: 完全可以，这就是"未必"最典型的用法——数字好，未必等于结果好。
ALEX: 一个正式名词，一个正式连接词，都是今天新闻里现成的例子。谢谢，Sam，中文部分就到这里。
SAM: Ngayon naman, Alex, dalawang salita mula sa balitang Pilipinas ngayon.
ALEX: Sige, simulan natin sa "gumaan."
SAM: Paano ito ginamit?
ALEX: Gumaan ang presyon sa piso sa nakaraang tatlong araw, dahil bumaba ang presyo ng langis bago pa ang biglaang pagtaas ngayong gabi.
SAM: Ano ang pagkakaiba nito sa "bumaba" o "humina"?
ALEX: Malaking pagkakaiba. Ang "bumaba" o "humina" ay tumutukoy lang sa numero mismo — bumaba ang halaga, humina ang piso. Ngunit ang "gumaan" ay tungkol sa pakiramdam ng presyon o bigat — parang may binuhat kang mabigat na bagay, at unti-unti itong naging magaan. Kaya kahit hindi pa lubos na nawala ang presyon sa piso, pwede mo pa ring sabihing gumaan ito, dahil bumawas ang bigat nito sa tatlong araw na iyon.
SAM: Kaya't ito ay tungkol sa relief, hindi resolution.
ALEX: Eksakto. At ito ang pangalawang salita, mula naman sa balita tungkol sa panahon: "pag-iingat."
SAM: Gamitin natin sa pangungusap.
ALEX: Nananawagan ang PAGASA ng pag-iingat sa mga lugar na maaaring apektuhan ng baha at pagguho ng lupa, habang tumatawid ang mababang presyon patungo sa West Philippine Sea.
SAM: Bakit hindi na lang "ingat" nang mag-isa?
ALEX: Magkaiba ang bigat. Ang "ingat" ay isang simpleng salita — pwede mong sabihin nang mabilis, kaswal, halos parang pabalita lamang. Ang "pag-iingat" naman ay mas pormal, mas may bigat — tumutukoy ito sa isang patuloy, sinasadyang gawain, hindi lang isang sandaling babala. Iyon din ang tono ng parehong PAGASA at ni Gobernador Remolona ngayon — hindi sila nagbibigay ng maikling babala lamang, kundi humihiling ng patuloy, sadyang pag-iingat sa parehong panahon at ekonomiya.
SAM: A word about relief without resolution, and a word about sustained caution rather than a passing warning — both straight from today's Philippines news.
ALEX: Four sharp ones today, two languages, all tied straight to the news.
