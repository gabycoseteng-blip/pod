## ENERGY / DATA CENTERS / UTILITIES — deep, practitioner-level (sourced from INBOX/Gmail — SemiAnalysis, Utility Dive)
1. **semianalysis-4hi-hbm-inference-cost-reversal** (Sept 13, "Long Live the Short King") — The decade-long trend of stacking ever-taller HBM (12-hi → 16-hi expected) has broken: Nvidia's next-gen Rubin Ultra drops to 192GB of HBM per GPU (from 288GB on Blackwell Ultra/standard Rubin) by moving to 8-hi stacks, and frontier-lab hardware teams are now pushing for 4-hi as the real optimum. Why: bandwidth per HBM cube is identical regardless of stack height (all 2048 I/Os split across dies), so a 4-hi stack delivers the same $/bandwidth as a 12-hi one at a fraction of the DRAM content — and DRAM/HBM wafers are the binding supply constraint (not enough 12-hi cubes exist for the N3/CoWoS capacity Nvidia has booked). SemiAnalysis's roofline model on a Rubin Ultra NVL576 system serving Kimi K3 (2.8T open-weight model) shows 8-hi and 12-hi deliver only 8% and 10% more peak throughput than 4-hi respectively, while costing 12.1% and 26.3% more in system BOM — meaning taller stacks are already higher cost-per-token at today's memory prices. A real InferenceX benchmark (16x GB300, Kimi K3, 85% vs 92% HBM utilization) showed throughput held up fine until concurrency crossed a threshold, then dropped ~30% as KVCache offload to DRAM kicked in 10x harder. Going to 4-hi could roughly double the usable HBM-cube supply, shifting the bottleneck to logic wafers, substrates, and packaging instead.
