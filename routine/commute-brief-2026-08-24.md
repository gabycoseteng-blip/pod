## DEEP DIVE — Adam Tooze, Chartbook 469 (newsletter, inbox-sourced)
"The Risk of Unwind — the US Treasury Market in the Era of the Hedge-Fund-Profit
Dollar" (Chartbook, Aug 23 2026, following Chartbook 468 on the March 2020
Treasury-market near-seizure).
- Old mechanism (foreign central banks recycling trade surpluses into UST) has
  largely collapsed since 2008; China is actively diversifying away from UST
  even as its trade surplus stays huge.
- New mechanism: leveraged hedge funds (concentrated in Cayman Islands funds)
  are now the marginal UST buyer. NY Fed's Phillip Monin: hedge fund UST
  holdings now EXCEED mutual funds' and US-chartered depository institutions'.
- Not real capital — it's borrowed: OFR data show hedge-fund repo borrowing +154%
  and prime-brokerage borrowing +83% since 2022.
- ~Half of hedge funds' $2.4T long UST position (Sept 2025) is leveraged
  arbitrage — chiefly the cash/futures "basis trade" that blew up March 2020
  and required ~$75bn/day of emergency Fed purchases to unwind.
- Basis-trade volume ~$830bn in Sept 2025 — nearly double its early-2020 peak —
  now 3.5% of privately-held UST by market value vs a 2.5% 2020 peak (+40% in
  concentration risk).
- Read: the world's "safest, most liquid" market is structurally dependent on
  leveraged, rate-sensitive, profit-driven positioning that already broke once
  and is now bigger. Ties directly to the show's long-yield-spike (30Y 5.28%
  8/19) and Bessent-buyback (8/20) coverage — this is the plumbing underneath.
- Attribution: "a feature in Adam Tooze's Chartbook."

   interconnection queue.

   centers)" (8/21). STEELMAN: data-center opposition polls ~80-20 against;

   and rate-hike concerns are real governance failures; fix utility-rate design
   and tax-abatement structures so host communities see the benefit (cites his

   functionally how projects die in practice (Georgia PSC delays, ERCOT queue
   fights this show has tracked all month); also assumes state utility
   commissions have redesign capacity most currently lack.
2. The Economist, leader, "Could AIs become conscious?" (8/20-22 issue).
   STEELMAN: even if no AI is conscious in any deep sense, growing numbers of
   people already treat chatbots as sentient (relationships, "AI psychosis"
   cases) — that mistaken belief alone could reshape law/welfare/relationships;
   build disclosure rules and anti-anthropomorphic design guardrails now as a
   precautionary hedge. COUNTER: leans on an outdated "only living things can
   be conscious" premise, sidestepping functionalist philosophy-of-mind
   literature; risks diverting attention from AI governance fights with
   harder, already-visible data (labor displacement, financial-stability
   exposure) toward a speculative one.
