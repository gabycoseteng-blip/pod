## DEEP DIVE
**Source (Path A — inbox newsletter)**: *Humanmaxxing* (Sublime's interview series, sublimeinternet@substack.com), interviewer Alex Dobrenko with guest Adam Mastroianni (Substack *Experimental History*; forthcoming book *The Strangest Idea in the World*). Published Aug 29, 2026.
Argument: LLMs have digested the whole internet and can cheaply produce anything derivable from consensus text — forces writers/thinkers toward genuine idiosyncrasy rather than synthesis. Good writing is generative, not retrieval: you don't know the ending when you start ("building a bridge over a chasm, one plank at a time"). Human work has "thickness" (rewards repeated attention); AI output is "thin" (looks fine until you look closely). Only humans can put something at stake — reputation, risk, a real stated memory; a machine can't lie about having been 13 once. Not anxious about this — sees it as a forcing function toward more distinctive, higher-taste work.
Why it matters: a clean, non-hedged theory of where economic value survives in white-collar/creative labor once generation is commoditized — attention and taste as the moat, not fluency. Demand-side counterweight to the show's usual capex/power framing of the AI story.
Backup: SCOTUS 5-4 (Aug 31) let Trump continue the White House ballroom construction on standing grounds, explicitly not ruling on legality — Roberts dissented with the three liberals, calling it "likely unlawful."
