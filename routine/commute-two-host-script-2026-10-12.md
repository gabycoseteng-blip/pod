## SEGMENT THREE — DEEP DIVE

SAM: Today's deep dive is a prize that is also an argument. On Friday the Norwegian Nobel Committee awarded the 2026 Peace Prize to Navi Pillay, the South African jurist. The committee's citation says it is for her efforts to promote peace and international law. This is not a newsletter or a viral thread: it is the single most contested piece of news of the week, so I wanted to give it room.
ALEX: Start with who she is, because the biography is the argument. Pillay was born on September 23rd, 1941 in Durban, the daughter of a bus driver, and grew up under white-minority rule. She studied at the University of Natal and later at Harvard, and in 1995 she became a judge of South Africa's High Court.
SAM: Then the international career, which is a tour of the architecture of post-1945 accountability. She was a judge and then president of the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda, a court associated with the landmark ruling that treated rape as an act of genocide. Then judge at the International Criminal Court from 2003 to 2008, and then UN High Commissioner for Human Rights from 2008 to 2014.
ALEX: The recent chapter is the one that is driving the reaction. From 2021 to 2025 she chaired the UN Commission of Inquiry on the Occupied Palestinian Territory, which concluded last year that Israel's actions in Gaza amounted to genocide. Israel rejects that finding.
SAM: And the reactions arrived within hours. Committee chair Jørgen Watne Frydnes called her one of the great defenders of international legal principles of our time. Antonio Guterres praised the choice. Trump called her an unabashed hater of America and Israel and said the committee had shamed itself beyond repair. Israel's Foreign Ministry called it a grotesque weaponization of an award.
ALEX: Here is my read. The Peace Prize has three modes. It can honor a peacemaker who ended a war, it can honor an activist who raised the cost of one, or it can honor an institution-builder. Pillay is the third. This is a prize for the idea that the powerful can be put in front of a judge, and that is exactly the idea under attack from Washington, and from Israel, and from a number of governments who prefer not to be asked questions.
SAM: The counterargument is serious, and it deserves to be said. Critics say a prize to someone who led a commission that Israel rejects is a political act dressed as a legal one, and that the committee has chosen a side in a live conflict. They also note that her investigative reports are not court judgments, and that the institutions she is associated with have struggled to enforce anything against a major power.
ALEX: That is fair, and I would add one more. The ICC has issued warrants it cannot execute, and the ICJ's orders depend on compliance. A prize does not fix enforcement. What it does is keep the norm alive for the next time a political window opens. Prizes to Andrei Sakharov and Liu Xiaobo did not free anyone quickly either, and the committee knew it.
SAM: Notice the framing from advocacy outlets, such as Democracy Now, which called it a stinging rebuke of Trump. I would be careful with that. The committee said nothing of the sort in its citation. The rebuke is something the reaction supplies, and the Trump statement made it one.
ALEX: She is the fifth South African to win the Peace Prize, and the ceremony is on December 10th in Oslo. The question I would watch is not what she says there. It is whether any government that signed the Rome Statute takes a visible step in the weeks before, because that is how you find out whether this was a rebuke or a eulogy.
SAM: And for markets listeners, the link is more direct than it sounds. Sanctions, asset freezes and shipping insurance are all downstream of international law being treated as binding. If that consensus erodes, every one of those risks reprices.

## SEGMENT FOUR — ENERGY / DATA CENTERS / UTILITIES

ALEX: The lead is a number that should make every PJM planner uncomfortable. The independent market monitor, Monitoring Analytics, told FERC in a September 25th filing that 4,016.9 megawatts of forecast data-center load had been cancelled or delayed as of September 21st. Forbes did the arithmetic: that is 42% of the data-center growth PJM had forecast for delivery years 2026/27 through 2028/29.
SAM: And the consequence is the gap. Strip that load out and the shortfall in the July auction falls from 6,831.3 megawatts to 3,055.0, a 55% reduction. The monitor's language is that forecast accuracy is highly questionable. Those percentages are Forbes' math rather than the monitor's own, so keep that in mind.
ALEX: Why it matters is the policy architecture built on top of the forecast. FERC suspended PJM's cost-allocation plan until February 2027 and set a hearing, and the Department of Energy wants a revised tariff by October 29th. If the backstop is sized to a load forecast that is nearly half phantom, then who pays for what is being decided on a number that might not materialize.
SAM: The practitioner's version is that load forecasts from utilities are a collection of duplicated requests. A developer files in three territories, and all three utilities count it. The monitor is saying the double-counting has a price tag.
ALEX: The second item is the other side of the same coin: what is actually getting built. PJM's Expedited Interconnection Track named its first 3 projects on October 8th, about 2.1 gigawatts in total. Engie has 860 megawatts of storage in Crawford, Pennsylvania and 800 megawatts in Morrow, Ohio, and LS Power is adding 455 megawatts to its 810 megawatt Hunterstown combined-cycle plant in Adams County, Pennsylvania. All are due online by mid-2029.
SAM: The headline is that the fast lane's first 2.1 gigawatts is mostly batteries and not gas. The rules matter here: projects over 250 megawatts, with a siting authority and an in-service date within 3 years, capped at 10 requests a year, and the track expires at the end of 2027. FERC approved it in June and the window opened July 31st. That is a very narrow door.
ALEX: Third, flexibility as a product. Voltus raised $225 million in a Series D led by Generation Investment Management, with Activate Capital and Vitol as co-leads. It wants to go from 8.5 gigawatts across more than 22,000 sites to 20 gigawatts by 2030, and the pitch is Bring Your Own Capacity: a data center pays to line up flexible demand from nearby homes and businesses.
SAM: The only data-center buyer named so far is Google, at up to 100 megawatts a year in PJM. I would treat that as a pilot, because the critique is obvious: a demand-response aggregator selling capacity is selling the promise of curtailment, and the test is whether it shows up on the worst afternoon of the summer.
ALEX: Fourth, a split in the data. A Latitude Media tracker counts 62 gigawatts of new supply approved, contracted or under construction by 26 vertically integrated utilities for data-center load, and 37.3 gigawatts of that, about two-thirds, is gas. Separately it counts roughly 72 gigawatts of planned onsite generation outside ERCOT across 33 projects, with gas on at least 39 gigawatts of it. I could not confirm those numbers independently, and another tracker, Cleanview, shows 56 gigawatts across 46 projects.
SAM: Both can be true, and the combination is the story. Utilities are choosing gas for the load they serve, and developers are choosing onsite gas to avoid the queue. The grid is being bypassed in one direction and overbuilt in the other.
ALEX: Last, a correction to the record on Google and Constellation. We covered the 890 megawatt uprate power purchase agreement. The part we left out is that it is a piece of a 3,590 megawatt arrangement in PJM: a separate 15-year agreement for 2,700 megawatts that is not tied to a specific plant. Constellation says it will invest more than $4.3 billion in its fleet, and the first upgraded plant delivers in 2028.
SAM: And the $4.3 billion is Constellation's capital spending, not Google's payment, and the price was not disclosed. That is the number to ask for. The unit-unspecified 2,700 megawatts is where the real capacity story sits, because the buyer is paying for an attribute and not an asset.
ALEX: The second-order read across all five: a forecast that may be half phantom, a fast lane that is mostly storage, flexibility sold as capacity, gas on both sides of the meter, and a hyperscaler contracting for capacity it cannot name. Everyone is buying optionality, and the bill for the options is what FERC is trying to allocate.

SAM: A word on the sourcing, since it matters for how much weight to put on this. Utility Dive, Latitude Media and the market monitor's own filing are the primary documents here, and several of the numbers reached me through secondary summaries, so check the filings before you quote them to a client.
ALEX: And one practical takeaway for developers: expect interconnection and load-forecast scrutiny to rise. If the monitor's argument gains traction, utilities will be asked for financial commitments, such as deposits and milestones, before load gets counted, and that favors projects with real sites, real equipment orders and a real offtaker.

## SEGMENT SEVEN — MANDARIN VOCAB

SAM: Two Mandarin words today, both tied to the China data due on Wednesday. Alex, start us off.
ALEX: The first is 未免, wèimiǎn. It means "rather too" or "a bit too," and it is a polite way to criticize. The speaker is saying something is excessive without quite saying it is wrong.
SAM: Give us the sentence.
ALEX: 现在就断定通胀已经见顶，未免太乐观了。(To conclude now that inflation has peaked is a bit too optimistic.)
ALEX: The pattern is almost always 未免, then 太 or 过于, then an adjective. It sits in the semi-formal register, 书面语 leaning, and you would use it in an editorial or a polite disagreement at work.
SAM: How does it differ from plain 太?
ALEX: 太 only says "too." 未免 adds a judgment, that the speaker finds the thing excessive. Say 太贵了 and you have stated a price. Say 未免太贵了 and you have raised an eyebrow.
SAM: The second word is 回暖, huínuǎn, literally "return to warm." It describes prices, demand or markets turning favorable again.
ALEX: The example: 随着出厂价格回暖，企业的利润开始改善。(As factory-gate prices warm up, corporate profits are starting to improve.) It collocates with 市场, 需求 and 价格, and it also works for the weather.
SAM: Compare it with 回升, which means a measurable rise. 回暖 is softer. It describes a trend turning and a mood improving, so it is the word you reach for before the numbers fully confirm it.
ALEX: Note that 未免 is the abstract word of the pair, and it pairs naturally with 回暖. 市场回暖的说法，未免言之过早。(The claim that the market is warming up is a bit premature.) Hold on to both for the China segment.

## SEGMENT TEN — OP-EDS & COMMENTARY

ALEX: First, George Will in the Washington Post, in a column summarized in coverage this weekend. I could not verify the exact date, so treat my summary as a summary. His argument, as reported, is that the Iran memorandum amounts to a bribe to Tehran and a betrayal of Israel, and that any final deal must be submitted to Congress.
