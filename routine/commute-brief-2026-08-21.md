## DEEP DIVE — source: inbox (Adam Tooze's Chartbook via Gmail)
Chartbook 466: "The Straits of Kaub and seven centuries of German political economy" — Adam Tooze, published 2026-08-14. Rhine carries 80% of Germany's ~223M tons/yr inland-shipped cargo; tightest chokepoint is a submerged rock ledge at Kaub, channel runs 20cm shallower than rest of river, forcing barges to lighten loads or halt in low water. Govt promised rock removal in 1992 under Kohl; 34yrs later, still not scheduled to finish until 2033 (shipping execs say actual engineering work is ~8 months). ING global macro head Carsten Brzeski calls Kaub "Germany's Strait of Hormuz." Contested fix: dredging (fast, politically attractive) doesn't add water to the system, actually speeds outflow to sea, worsening downstream shortages in future dry years — vs water-agency's preferred fix, low-water groynes narrowing the channel to concentrate existing water. Federal funding: €7M sediment-replenishment elsewhere on Rhine; €10.5M to convert just 3 of thousands of cargo vessels to shallow-draft low-emission design — "a gesture rather than the beginning of a fleet renewal." Ties to throughline: local fix vs system-wide worsening, physical/political bottlenecks binding faster than capital can move.
