## SEGMENT THREE — DEEP DIVE
SAM: Our deep dive comes from The Economist, which ran a piece this week headlined Grand Theft Auto VI may mark the end of an era in gaming. The argument, as The Economist frames it: GTA VI, which came out yesterday, is the most eagerly awaited video game in history and may be the biggest entertainment release of any kind. It may also be the last such blockbuster, because studios face shrinking incentives to make top-tier titles.
ALEX: Let's start with how big, because the scale is the setup. The game launched on November 19, 13 years after GTA V, after slipping from fall 2025 to May 2026 and then to November. It's on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series only, no PC yet, at 79.99 dollars, or 99.99 for the Ultimate edition. Metacritic has it at 99. Sensor Tower estimates 24.58 million copies and about 2.36 billion dollars in the first 24 hours, roughly 19 million of them preorders and about 75% on PlayStation. GTA V did 11.21 million copies and 815.7 million dollars in its first day. Take-Two hasn't confirmed any number, it just calls demand unprecedented, so treat those as third-party estimates.
SAM: And the cost of getting there?
ALEX: Never disclosed. Estimates run from 1 to 2 billion dollars, against roughly 265 million for GTA V. That's the core of The Economist's thesis. GTA V has sold about 230 million copies, plus a decade of GTA Online revenue, and that's what Take-Two is betting a billion-plus on: not a hit, but a 10-year platform. Its guidance for the fiscal year to March is 8 to 8.2 billion dollars of net bookings, up from about 6.7 billion. And the stock did the classic thing: up 6.2% to 216.61 on the reviews Wednesday, then down about 3.7% on launch day as hedge funds sold the news.
SAM: So why would the biggest launch ever be the end of anything?
ALEX: Because almost nobody else can do this maths. Jason Schreier at Bloomberg puts a standard AAA budget at 300 million dollars or more now. The UK's competition regulator estimated 90 to 180 million dollars to develop a top title, plus 50 to 150 million to market it. Breaking even needs 6 million copies or more at 70 dollars before marketing. And the audience isn't growing to match. Newzoo's data has new releases capturing only about 12 to 13% of playtime on PC and console, flat for 3 years, while about 54% goes to games that are 6 or more years old. Fortnite, Minecraft, Roblox, Call of Duty. Players are already busy.
SAM: Which is a winner-take-all market, not a dying one.
ALEX: That's the right pushback, and I think it's the real reading. Look at what's happened to everyone in the middle. Amir Satvat's tracker counts 15,631 games-industry layoffs in 2024, 9,197 in 2025, and 10,977 this year through October 20, about 58,000 since 2022, with something like 35 to 40 studios closed this year. Ubisoft shut 2 studios and cancelled 6 games. And hardware is getting more expensive, not cheaper: Sony raised the PS5 by 100 dollars to 649.99 in April, its second increase in under a year, blamed on memory prices, which are being driven up by AI data centres.
SAM: That's a link I didn't expect. The AI build-out is making the console more expensive.
ALEX: It's the same DRAM. Every high-bandwidth memory wafer that goes to an accelerator is a wafer that doesn't go to consumer electronics, so the installed base grows slower and the addressable market for a 300-million-dollar game shrinks. Meanwhile the user-generated platforms aren't a safe harbour either: Roblox's daily users fell 9% to 138 million in the third quarter, its first annual decline.
SAM: Then the case against The Economist. Small games are doing fine.
ALEX: Better than fine. Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, a French game made on a fraction of a AAA budget, sold more than 8 million copies and swept the game-of-the-year awards. Capcom's Resident Evil Requiem sold 5 million in 5 days on a mid-sized budget. So the alternative reading is that what's ending isn't the hit, it's the 300-million-dollar game that isn't GTA. Budgets bifurcate: a handful of 1-billion-dollar franchises that function as platforms, and a lot of disciplined 30-to-80-million-dollar games, with very little in between.
SAM: And the AI angle?
ALEX: Ben Feder, a former Take-Two chief executive, called GTA VI the last pre-AI blockbuster in Fortune. Strauss Zelnick says generative AI played zero part in it. The optimistic view is that AI tools collapse art and testing costs and revive the middle. The sceptical view is that AI is already raising the cost of the hardware people play on. My read: The Economist is right that GTA VI is unrepeatable, but for a narrower reason. Not because blockbusters stop working, but because only a franchise that can earn for a decade justifies a 13-year build. That's a lesson for every capital-intensive bet being made right now, including the ones you work on: scale wins, but only when the asset keeps earning long after launch day.

## SEGMENT SEVEN — MANDARIN VOCAB
SAM: Now Mandarin, with 2 items for this morning's China story on PDD and Alibaba.
ALEX: The first is an abstract adverb of surprise: 竟然, jìngrán, meaning unexpectedly, or to one's surprise. It's HSK 4, and it goes right before the verb or adjective to signal that the outcome defied what you'd expect. Example: 收入没有增长，利润竟然涨了25%。(Revenue didn't grow, yet profit unexpectedly rose 25%.)
SAM: Contrast it with 居然 and 没想到. 居然 means almost the same thing but leans spoken and often carries a note of disapproval or disbelief, as in 他居然没来, he actually didn't show up. 竟然 is slightly more neutral and more at home in writing and news reports. 没想到, didn't expect, is the plain colloquial version and sits at the front of the clause rather than before the verb: 没想到市场跌得这么快。(Nobody expected the market to fall this fast.) The register tip: in a finance headline, 竟然 adds editorial surprise, so analysts use it sparingly.
ALEX: The second is 压缩, yāsuō. Literally to compress, as in compressing a file, but in business Chinese it means to squeeze or cut costs, and it's formal, written register. It collocates with 成本, costs, 开支, spending, 预算, budget, and 利润空间, profit margin. Example: 拼多多压缩了营销费用，利润反而增长了。(PDD squeezed its marketing spending, and profit actually rose.)
SAM: The near-synonyms: 减少 is neutral, just to reduce, and works for almost anything. 削减 is stronger, to slash, often for government budgets or jobs. 压缩 has the image of pressure, squeezing something into a smaller space, so it suits operating costs that are being tightened, not eliminated. In casual speech you'd say 省钱, to save money.
ALEX: Listen for both in the China story next.

## SEGMENT TEN — OP-EDS & COMMENTARY
SAM: Op-eds. Hal Brands, in Bloomberg Opinion, argues that proxy wars are the future of US, China and Russia relations. His case: the Shenzhen summit and the talk of a new Yalta hide relations that are still deeply hostile. The 3 powers are already fighting each other through proxies, in Ukraine, in Iran and the Gulf, and around the Taiwan Strait, and America's allies are right to fear side deals that carve up the world over their heads.
ALEX: The steelman is strong: Russian drones built on Iranian designs with Chinese components, North Korean soldiers in Russia, Chinese payment rails helping everyone dodge sanctions. The Cold War had both proxy wars and arms control, and the arms control is why it stayed cold.
SAM: Then Daniel Moss, also in Bloomberg Opinion, on what Deutsche Bank calls the chip dollar. His argument: AI capital spending is pulling global savings into American tech, which makes the dollar stronger but also more volatile, and leaves every other economy more exposed to sudden US policy and technology shocks.
ALEX: It's a good frame for this week. The dollar at its strongest since April 2025, the yen near 160, the peso and the rupiah under pressure, central banks from Jakarta to Pretoria hiking or nearly hiking to keep up. The counter is that dominance resting on 1 hot sector is fragile by definition. Thursday's Nasdaq reversal, Nvidia at a 52-week low, an IPO fading after hours: if the AI trade unwinds, the same flows go out the way they came in. The chip dollar may describe a cyclical peak rather than a new regime, and for Asia that's the more dangerous version, because you hike to defend against inflows that then reverse.
