# DEEP DIVE — Economist/1843: "Pinot Noir: inside the criminal gangs making fake wine" (William Ralston, pub ~11/6, 1843 feature; Economist Today 11/7; Longreads repost 11/10). Path: Economist inbox + web. Full text not accessible; facts below come from the underlying record.
- Argument: wine fraud is ancient, but gangs are making more and better fakes and moving down-market, from trophy Burgundy to supermarket brands like Yellow Tail.
- F1: Precedent "Red Bicyclette": 12 Languedoc producers/dealers (Sieur d'Arques, Ducasse) sold ~18M bottles' worth (some say 16M) of Merlot/Syrah blend as pure Pinot Noir to E&J Gallo, 2006-08.
- F2: It was caught by a routine audit: Sieur d'Arques sold more Pinot than it bought. Convictions came in 2010 with suspended sentences of 1-6 months plus fines, and a US class action followed.
- F3: Oct 2024 Europol-backed FR/IT/CH bust: 6 arrests, fake PDO French wine sold at up to ~€15k ($16k)/bottle, >€2M netted.
- F4: Yellow Tail: a brand source alleged a Moldovan gang made up to 100,000 cases. Expert Maureen Downey alleges an Asian gang partnered with a European one.
- F5: UK enforcement is local. A Sutton Coldfield shop lost its licence over 41 fake bottles, and fakes were sold ~£2/case below cash-and-carry.
- F6: UK FSA National Food Crime Unit: an arrest on 3 Mar 2026 over ~£500k of fake wine and prosecco.
- F7: Rudy Kurniawan: >$20M of fake top vintages by conservative counts, vs Downey's ~$550M estimate.
- F8: Italy: a separate scandal involved >=70M litres of cheap wine adulterated with acid, manure and fertiliser.
- F9: Countermeasures: holograms, per-bottle digital codes, colour-shift inks. Pétrus uses ink from the supplier of US dollar-bill ink.
- F10: Scale: an oft-quoted "20% of internationally traded wine is fraudulent" (French press) vs Downey's 5-10% of the secondary market. Both are weakly sourced.
- Contested: true prevalence; whether mass-market fakes are driven by tariffs/excise or by organised-crime diversification; how much harm a "fake" Pinot does when nobody can taste the difference.
- 2nd-order: the economics flip. At £6/bottle, margin comes from volume and excise evasion, not connoisseurs, so it is a customs/supply-chain problem, not an auction-house one. Provenance tech, aka serialisation, gets pushed onto cheap SKUs.
- Backup: Economist 11/7 "Is childlessness contagious?" ("Generation Maybe" book; Dutch register study in Demography 2020: colleagues' births raise own fertility, and removing peer effects cuts pregnancies ~5.8%).
