## ENERGY / DATA CENTERS — source: inbox newsletters (DealFlow.energy weekly Sept 14-20; Utility Dive Weekender Sept 19; SemiAnalysis)
LEAD ARGUMENT — "The Last 10%" guest column, David Riester (Founder/Managing Partner, Segue Sustainable Infrastructure), DealFlow.energy, published this week:
- Thesis: solar+storage covers ~90% of a large load's annual energy cheaply and fast — batteries solve the diurnal problem well. Pushing 90%→95% roughly doubles plan cost; 95%→100% is a further multiple. The last 10% is clustered in correlated, seasonal, system-wide-scarce hours (e.g. a run of overcast January days) where everyone is short simultaneously.
- The mechanism breaking clean buildout: load-serving entities won't sell "the last 10%" firming/shaping as a standard tariffed product — no obligation, no shelf product. So hyperscalers, wanting certainty, build the WHOLE load themselves behind the meter — sized for the worst 25-50 hours/year. Result: a gas plant with the SAME nameplate capacity as the data center (~10x what the actual gap required), at a capacity factor that "would embarrass a peaker," and the clean assets get shrunk/deferred.
- Riester's fix: a "residual-firming tariff" — once a load demonstrably secures ≥80% of annual energy from deliverable resources under defined performance criteria, it can buy firming for the remainder at an openly-priced, resource-neutral, tariffed premium (storage of any duration, demand response, imports, existing utility fleet — gas only if genuinely cheapest, but visible/tariffed at 10% scale rather than hidden behind a fence at 100% scale). No new statute needed — FERC's pending large-load interconnection dockets are the natural vehicle; states/PUCs hold LSEs to naming and honoring the price.
