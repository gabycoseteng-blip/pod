## SEGMENT SEVEN — MANDARIN VOCAB
SAM: Two Mandarin words before China, both tied to today's story about a very uneven profit report.
ALEX: First one's a connective, and it's the kind of construction you'd really only hear in writing or a formal news broadcast — 并非…而是…, bìngfēi ... ér shì ....
SAM: It literally corrects a false impression — "it's not actually X, it's really Y." Plainer Mandarin would just use 不是…而是…, which is neutral, just stating a substitution. 并非 adds an argumentative edge — you're specifically pushing back on something someone might reasonably believe.
ALEX: Here's how it lands in a sentence: 工业利润的回升并非内需真正好转，而是人工智能产业链价格上涨所致。(The pickup in industrial profit isn't really domestic demand recovering — it's the AI supply chain's price surge that's doing it.)
SAM: That's exactly the move a finance-desk analyst makes on a call — "don't be fooled by the headline number, here's what's actually driving it." Very 书面语, very formal register.
ALEX: Second word: 分化, fēnhuà — to diverge, to split apart, to stratify.
SAM: It's an abstract word, not a physical thing — you'd pair it with 行业 (an industry) or 效益 (performance): 电子行业与传统制造业的利润出现明显分化。(Profit performance has clearly diverged between electronics and traditional manufacturing.)
ALEX: The near-synonym to watch is 分裂, fēnliè — that one implies an actual rupture, a break, like a political split. 分化 is softer and more gradual — things that used to move together, slowly drifting apart, without anything actually breaking.
SAM: Both words are built for exactly the story we're about to get into — a number that looks fine on the surface hiding two very different economies underneath it.
