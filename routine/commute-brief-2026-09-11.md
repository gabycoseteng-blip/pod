## OP-EDS & COMMENTARY

- Noah Smith (Noahpinion, Sept 9), "AI keeps stubbornly refusing to take our
  jobs" — Steelman: aggregate US labor market stays near record health
  despite rising Ipsos/Pew fear of AI displacing (not complementing) labor;
  automation historically creates jobs as often as destroys them; the
  job-loss narrative outruns the data. Counter: aggregate stats mask
  concentrated, fast displacement in specific white-collar functions
  (coding, customer service, junior analyst work) not yet visible in
  top-line data because capex/deployment cycles lag 12-18 months — "no mass
  layoffs yet" isn't "won't happen."
- Liam Denning (Bloomberg Opinion, Sept 10), "Bashing Ford's China Ties Is a
  Recipe for Obsolescence" — Steelman: Transport Sec. Sean Duffy's attack on
  Ford's CATL licensing deal (LFP batteries, Marshall MI plant) misreads the
  risk — cutting Ford off from Chinese battery tech doesn't reduce
  dependence, just leaves US automakers with worse/costlier batteries while
  Chinese rivals keep improving. Counter: licensing IP from a strategic
  rival's national champion is exactly the dependency that becomes a lever
  in a future crisis (see rare earths) — economic security is a legitimate
  policy variable, not just a cost curve.
