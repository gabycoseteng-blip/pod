SAM: And it's an adaptive platform design, so multiple candidates can run inside the same study. Ervebo goes in first, even though it's not proven against Bundibugyo, and two Bundibugyo-specific candidates, from Moderna and from Oxford, are expected to join later.
ALEX: The ethical and scientific debate is right there. SAM: The candidate pipeline is moving in parallel, too. ALEX: And that is the timing mismatch that decides outcomes. ## SEGMENT SEVEN — MANDARIN VOCAB

SAM: Time for the Mandarin words. Today we have two, one connective and one abstract collocation, both of which show up constantly in financial and policy writing. The first is 况且.
ALEX: 况且 means "besides" or "moreover," and it's a mildly formal connective. The key to the 况且 is that it adds an extra supporting reason after you've already given a first one. It doesn't compare, it stacks. Here's an example. 央行已经注入了大量资金，况且债券收益率还在创新低。 (The central bank has already injected a large amount of money, and besides, bond yields keep hitting new lows.)
SAM: And how is that different from 何况, which we've covered before?
ALEX: 何况 is more rhetorical, it usually frames an "even more so" argument, like "if that's hard, how much harder is this." 况且 simply adds one more reason, so it's calmer and it fits analysis writing better. The usual position is at the beginning of the second clause, often after a comma, with the first reason already given.
SAM: Another sentence. 这个项目成本很高，况且回报期也很长。 (This project has high costs, and besides, the payback period is long too.)
ALEX: The second word is 与否, and it's the abstract one. 与否 means "whether or not," and it's formal and written. It attaches after a noun or a verb to say "X or not X," as in 成功与否, success or not.
SAM: How is that different from 是否, which is the one most learners already know?
ALEX: 是否 is a question word that goes before the verb. 是否成功 is "whether it succeeds." 与否 goes after, and it turns the whole thing into a noun phrase you can treat as a subject. So 成功与否 can be the subject of the sentence, which is why it sits so naturally in headlines and editorials.
SAM: Show us. 刺激政策成功与否，取决于居民愿不愿意消费。 (Whether or not the stimulus policy succeeds depends on whether households are willing to spend.)
ALEX: And one more. 能否实现目标，关键不在于资金多少，而在于需求恢复与否。 (Whether the goal can be achieved depends not on how much money there is, but on whether demand recovers or not.) You'll hear both of these words in the China segment coming up next.
SAM: Remember, 况且 for adding a second reason, and 与否 for turning an either-or into a noun phrase.
