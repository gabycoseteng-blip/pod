## (A) DEEP DIVE (science): First radio signal traced to an exoplanet -- Beta Pictoris b
THESIS: A repeating, polarized radio burst has been pinned to one named planet 64 ly away, which would give the first DIRECT magnetic-field measurement of an exoplanet -- but it is a preprint, not peer reviewed, and the authors call it a strong candidate.
FACTS
- Preprint: "Discovery of radio emission from the exoplanet beta Pictoris b", arXiv 2609.16720, posted ~Sept 15 2026 (per IBTimes/secondary).
- Team: Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian + University of Oregon.
- Instrument: MeerKAT array, South Africa; system observed on 4 occasions across 2025 and 2026.
- Signal: rapid, recurring, strongly circularly polarized bursts PLUS persistent emission, ~0.85-3.5 GHz.
- Mechanism claimed: electron cyclotron maser instability (ECMI) = same process behind auroral radio from Earth, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune and some cold brown dwarfs.
- Magnetic field: ECMI emits near the electron gyrofrequency, so a burst up to 3.5 GHz implies >= ~1.25 kilogauss (1,250 G) at the emission site.
- Comparison: Earth surface ~0.5 G; Jupiter strongest regions ~14 G (so roughly 90x Jupiter's peak).
- Planet: young gas giant, ~12 Jupiter masses, ~64 light-years away; a directly imaged planet (planet is a known target, orbits a young star with a debris disk -- background).
- "First": all prior exoplanet radio claims were not unambiguously localized to a planet; this one is claimed localized (that's the novelty, per Harvard/Oregon framing).
- Prior art: an earlier search for auroral radio from beta Pic b exists (arXiv 2312.15176, 2023) -- earlier hunt, now apparently rewarded.
- Viral: went viral Sept 26; a Polymarket post drew ~41K views in first hours (via explainx.ai, secondary; alien-hype angle; IBTimes headline: "disappoint alien enthusiasts" -- it's physics, not ET).
- Press pickup: Newsweek, IBTimes, Yahoo/Gadget Review, Techlicious, Sci.News, Ground News.
- Flux (mJy), burst duration, significance in sigma: [UNVERIFIED -- not in accessible sources].
CONTESTED
- Not peer reviewed; authors themselves say candidate. Open questions I can state as questions, not findings: could emission come from the host star or a background source; would a 12 Mjup object be better classed as a brown dwarf-like body (brown dwarfs already show ECMI) -- if so, "first planet" is partly semantic.
- Inferred field depends on ECMI interpretation holding.
SECOND-ORDER READ
- Magnetic fields shield atmospheres from stellar wind -> radio is a possible route to judging habitability of FAR smaller worlds later; Newsweek headline frames it as boosting habitable-world search. Caveat: a 12-Jupiter-mass giant says little about rocky planets.
- Method is cheap: radio telescopes (MeerKAT, SKA precursors) can screen planets that optical can't. Watch for independent replication / a second telescope.

### 1. Allison Schrager, Bloomberg Opinion, Sept 30: "The 'Intangible Economy' Is Making Everyone Miserable"
STEELMAN
- Paradox: Americans have never been richer, living standards and life expectancy at highs, yet the economy "feels broken."
- Thesis (quote): "our economy is increasingly built on intangibles, and it may be hard to feel satisfied when so much of what we have is no longer in the physical realm"; few things we own are physical anymore.
- Labor too: more people in services, building service skills; jobs pay more and are less physically arduous, but "there is no output from our labor" you can point to.
- She concedes intangibles are why we're richer and live longer: tech rewards knowledge over physical labor.
- Implication: some of the gloom is psychological/structural, not just prices or policy.
COUNTER: Unfalsifiable vibes -- gloom is better explained by housing, healthcare and childcare costs and the recent yield/inflation squeeze (already aired), which are very tangible; and people felt this angst about factory closures too. "Intangible" also flatters policymakers: the fix is therapy, not affordability.

STEELMAN
- Will says Marriott's book on the death of reading may not be pessimistic ENOUGH.
- Marriott's premise: the book -- the artifact behind skepticism, rationality, science, democracy -- is of rapidly dwindling importance; post-literacy = reading/writing lose importance and people lose ability or will to use words "consciously and responsibly."

Podcasts/audio are still language-heavy; the decline in long-form book reading is real but "mystical, tribal, autocratic" is a leap -- autocracies were literate too.
