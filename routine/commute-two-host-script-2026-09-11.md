## SEGMENT SEVEN — MANDARIN VOCAB

SAM: Two Mandarin words before China — one's a comparison connective, the other's the exact abstract concept behind today's story.

ALEX: First: 相比之下 — xiāngbǐ zhīxià. It means "by comparison" or "in contrast" — a connective you use to set up an explicit comparison between two things you've just described. It works in both written and spoken register, though it leans slightly more formal than just saying 但是, "but."

SAM: How is it different from just using 但是 or 然而?

ALEX: 但是 and 然而 both signal a general contrast or contradiction. 相比之下 specifically sets up a side-by-side comparison — you need two concrete things already on the table, and it's saying "now look at them next to each other." It's the connective a finance desk reaches for right before putting two numbers against each other.

SAM: Second word — and this one names the actual thing regulators are testing for in today's story.

ALEX: 透明度 — tòumíngdù. It means "transparency" — literally 透明, transparent or see-through, plus 度, a suffix meaning degree or extent. It's an abstract noun, used constantly in regulatory and governance contexts to talk about how much a company or process discloses.

SAM: Is there a near-synonym I should watch for?

ALEX: 公开性, gōngkāixìng, is close — "openness" — but it leans more toward the act of making something public, while 透明度 is about the quality or degree of clarity itself, which is why regulators specifically measure and require a certain 透明度 rather than a certain 公开性.

SAM: Put the two together the way you'd actually hear this on a finance desk?

ALEX: 相比之下，DeepSeek的估值远低于OpenAI，但监管要求的透明度可能更高。 ("By comparison, DeepSeek's valuation is far lower than OpenAI's, but the transparency regulators require may be higher.") That's essentially the sentence behind today's China story.
