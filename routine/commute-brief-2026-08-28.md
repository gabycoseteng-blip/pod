## DEEP DIVE — PRIMARY (fresh territory, non-macro)
Betelgeuse's hidden companion star, directly imaged — ends a 100-year search. Team led by Miguel Montargès (Observatoire de Paris), published in Astronomy & Astrophysics, wide pickup Aug 27. ESO Very Large Telescope + SPHERE instrument imaged Dec 2024, timed to predicted max separation of a hypothesized companion ("Betelgeuse B") proposed ~100 years ago to explain Betelgeuse's brightness variability. Result: clearest-ever direct image; mass = 2-3 solar masses (not ~1 as assumed) — a real, hot, young B-type star, visible precisely BECAUSE it's more massive/brighter than sensitivity models predicted ("too faint to detect" — wrong). Orbit ~6 years; companion now entering receding phase, returns to observable quadrature late 2027 (confirmation window). Reopens questions about what triggered Betelgeuse's 2019-2020 "Great Dimming" and how a companion affects a supernova progenitor's pre-explosion evolution. [ESO, ScienceDaily, Sky & Telescope, Space.com]
