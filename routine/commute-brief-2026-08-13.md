## OP-EDS & COMMENTARY (1-2, steelman + counter)
OPED 1: David Wallace-Wells w/ Yale economist Natasha Sarin, NYT Opinion — "Why the U.S. Economy Needs A.I. — Bubble or Not" (Aug 12, 2026)
STEELMAN: AI is simultaneously a real technology story and a real financial bubble — Anthropic and OpenAI both beat revenue targets in July (genuine demand), but the buildout is financed with leverage precarious enough that a hedge fund betting on imminent AGI (Situational Awareness, run by 24-year-old Leopold Aschenbrenner) nearly collapsed on 4x leverage when markets wobbled. Sarin: "the markets can remain irrational longer than you can stay solvent."
COUNTER: An economy whose growth depends on a debt-financed speculative boom staying inflated isn't being saved by AI, it's hostage to it — the "too big to fail, too big to correct" logic that turned a real technology into 2008. If underlying revenue is genuinely strong, it should stand without the leveraged wrapper; if it can't, calling the leverage "necessary" just launders the risk.
source: NYT Opinion (dnyuz.com syndication)

OPED 2: Noah Smith, Noahpinion Substack — "Banning Data Centers Would Blow Up the U.S. Economy" (Aug 8, 2026)
STEELMAN: A bipartisan backlash against data centers (Wisconsin gubernatorial candidate Francesca Hong; moratoriums in blue New York and red Texas) targets the one thing propping up US growth. Smith concedes the long-run power/water competition worry is legitimate but argues an abrupt ban now, with nowhere else to turn for growth, risks self-inflicted collapse; also argues water-usage panic is overblown since cooling water is recirculated, not consumed.
COUNTER: Calling data centers "the engine keeping the economy afloat" is itself an admission the growth is dangerously narrow and concentrated — and elides who pays for that engine's fuel: households already absorbing surcharges as FirstEnergy/Dominion push gigawatt-scale interconnection costs onto ratepayers. The real choice was never "ban vs. nothing" — it was targeted permitting/cost-allocation reform vs. a status quo externalizing the bill.
source: noahpinion.blog
